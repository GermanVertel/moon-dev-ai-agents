STRATEGY_NAME: VolatilityContrarian

STRATEGY_DETAILS:

This strategy exploits the predictive information embedded in Option Implied Volatility (OIV) to identify contrarian long-short opportunities arising from divergent sector rotations. The core thesis is that elevated OIV in a sector signals fear/uncertainty that often precedes mean reversion, while depressed OIV signals complacency that precedes reversal — making OIV a contrarian signal for sector rotation timing.

1. KEY STRATEGY COMPONENTS

a) OIV Signal Construction
- For each sector ETF (or basket of stocks), compute a sector-level OIV metric, e.g., a cap-weighted average of 30-day ATM implied volatility across constituents.
- Normalize OIV into a z-score relative to its own trailing 252-day history: OIV_z = (OIV_current − mean_252) / std_252.
- Compute cross-sectional dispersion of OIV_z across sectors to detect "divergent sector rotations" — periods where sectors are moving in opposite volatility regimes.

b) Divergence Detection
- Rank sectors by OIV_z. High positive z = fear-driven dislocation (candidate long). High negative z = complacency (candidate short).
- Require divergence confirmation: the spread between top-quartile and bottom-quartile OIV_z must exceed a threshold (e.g., > 1.5 std of historical dispersion) to trigger positioning.
- Optional confirmation via realized vs. implied volatility spread (variance risk premium) to filter noise.

c) Directional Logic (Contrarian)
- Long sectors with extreme high OIV_z (oversold/fearful, expected to revert up).
- Short sectors with extreme low OIV_z (overbought/complacent, expected to revert down).
- Only act when sector price momentum is decelerating or reversing, to avoid catching falling knives.

2. ENTRY / EXIT RULES

Entry:
- Enter when OIV_z crosses ±1.5 to ±2.0 AND cross-sectional dispersion exceeds threshold.
- Long top-ranked (highest OIV_z) sector(s); short bottom-ranked (lowest OIV_z) sector(s).
- Rebalance weekly or bi-weekly based on signal decay.

Exit:
- Close long when OIV_z reverts toward 0 (e.g., |OIV_z| < 0.5).
- Close short when OIV_z rises back toward neutral or dispersion collapses.
- Hard stop if sector price moves adversely beyond a pre-set ATR multiple (e.g., 2× ATR).

3. RISK MANAGEMENT

a) Position Sizing by Inverse OIV
- Weight each position inversely to its OIV multiple: w_i ∝ 1 / OIV_i (or 1 / OIV_z magnitude).
- This scales down exposure to high-volatility (high-OIV) names and up to low-volatility names, equalizing risk contribution.
- Cap single-sector weight (e.g., ≤ 15%) and enforce gross/net exposure limits.

b) Dollar/Beta Neutrality
- Maintain market-neutral or beta-neutral construction by matching long and short beta exposures.
- Rebalance beta hedge weekly.

c) Stop-Loss & Drawdown Controls
- Per-position stop: 2× ATR or 8% adverse move.
- Portfolio drawdown circuit breaker: reduce gross exposure by 50% if drawdown exceeds 10%.
- Monitor OIV term structure (contango/backwardation) as a regime filter; stand down in extreme backwardation.

d) Liquidity & Execution
- Only trade sectors with adequate options liquidity (tight bid-ask spreads, deep OI).
- Use VWAP/TWAP execution to minimize slippage on rebalances.

4. REQUIRED INDICATORS / DATA

- Option Implied Volatility (30-day ATM) per sector and constituent.
- OIV z-score (252-day rolling) and cross-sectional OIV dispersion.
- Realized volatility (for variance risk premium comparison).
- Sector price momentum (e.g., 1M/3M returns) and ATR for stops.
- Sector betas (for neutrality) and correlation matrix (for diversification checks).
- Options liquidity metrics: bid-ask spread, open interest, volume.

SUMMARY: VolatilityContrarian combines OIV z-scores, cross-sectional dispersion, and inverse-OIV position sizing to systematically trade mean reversion across divergent sector rotations, with strict risk controls and beta-neutral construction.
