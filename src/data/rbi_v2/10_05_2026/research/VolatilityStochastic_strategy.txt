STRATEGY_NAME: VolatilityStochastic

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy combines long-term volatility regime analysis with stochastic oscillator timing to capture mean-reversion moves after extended volatility expansions. The core premise: when volatility reaches extreme long-term levels (either very high or very low), price tends to revert, and the stochastic oscillator provides precise entry/exit timing within that reversal.

1. KEY STRATEGY COMPONENTS
- Long-Term Volatility Filter: Use a long lookback (e.g., 100–200 periods) on ATR or standard deviation of returns to identify when volatility is at historical extremes (top/bottom 10–20 percentile of its range).
- Regime Detection: 
  - High-Volatility Regime: volatility above upper threshold → expect sharp reversals after exhaustion.
  - Low-Volatility Regime: volatility below lower threshold → expect breakout/reversion after compression.
- Stochastic Oscillator (14,3,3 or 21,5,5 for longer-term bias): Used to confirm overbought/oversold conditions aligned with the volatility extreme.
- Confirmation: Only act when stochastic crosses out of extreme zones (below 20 for longs, above 80 for shorts) AND volatility is at an extreme.

2. ENTRY RULES
Long Entry:
- Long-term volatility (ATR/StdDev over 100–200 bars) is in the bottom or top decile of its historical range.
- Stochastic %K crosses above %D from below 20 (oversold reversal).
- Price is above a long-term moving average (e.g., 200 SMA) OR has shown a clear capitulation wick.
- Enter on close of the confirmation candle.

Short Entry:
- Long-term volatility at an extreme (high or low).
- Stochastic %K crosses below %D from above 80 (overbought reversal).
- Price is below the 200 SMA OR shows a blow-off top wick.
- Enter on close of the confirmation candle.

3. EXIT RULES
- Take Profit: Fixed R-multiple (1.5R–2R) or when stochastic reaches the opposite extreme (80 for longs, 20 for shorts).
- Stop Loss: Placed beyond the recent swing high/low or at 1× ATR from entry (volatility-adjusted).
- Trailing Exit: Optional — trail using the stochastic %D line; exit when %K crosses %D against the position.
- Time Stop: Exit if no reversal materializes within N bars (e.g., 20) as volatility may be trending, not reverting.

4. RISK MANAGEMENT
- Position sizing inversely proportional to current ATR (volatility parity): smaller size in high-vol regimes, larger in low-vol regimes.
- Max risk per trade: 0.5–1% of equity.
- Avoid trading during known news events or earnings that could invalidate volatility assumptions.
- Cap concurrent positions and correlate exposure across instruments.
- Use hard stops — never widen them; volatility reversals can fail violently.

5. REQUIRED INDICATORS
- ATR (100–200 period) or rolling standard deviation of returns for long-term volatility.
- Percentile ranking of current volatility vs. historical range.
- Stochastic Oscillator (14,3,3 or 21,5,5) with %K and %D.
- 200-period SMA (or EMA) for trend bias.
- Optional: Bollinger Band Width (long period) as an alternate volatility compression/expansion gauge.

6. NOTES / EDGE CASES
- Best suited for ranging or post-impulse markets, not strong trends.
- Works across timeframes; longer timeframes (daily/weekly) give stronger signals.
- Backtest volatility percentile thresholds — 10/90 vs 20/80 materially changes trade frequency.
- Combine with volume confirmation (climax volume at extremes) for higher win rate.
