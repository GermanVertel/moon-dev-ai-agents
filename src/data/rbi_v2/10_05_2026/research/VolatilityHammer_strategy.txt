STRATEGY_NAME: VolatilityHammer

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy combines volatility-based filtering with candlestick reversal patterns to sell put options at expiration. The core premise is that elevated VIX levels (>20) indicate high implied volatility and elevated option premiums, making short puts attractive for income generation. The Hammer candlestick pattern signals a potential bullish reversal, suggesting downside momentum is exhausting.

KEY STRATEGY COMPONENTS:
1. Volatility Filter: VIX must be above 20 at the time of entry (or at least on the entry day).
2. Candlestick Pattern: A Hammer candlestick must form on the underlying asset (e.g., SPY, QQQ, or individual stock) on the daily chart.
3. Expiration Timing: Sell put options that expire at the next available expiration (weekly or monthly) to capture elevated premium.
4. Underlying Selection: Liquid, optionable assets with tight bid-ask spreads and sufficient open interest.

ENTRY RULES:
- Confirm VIX > 20. If VIX is below 20, no trade.
- Identify a Hammer candlestick on the daily chart of the underlying. A Hammer has:
  - Small real body at the top of the range.
  - Long lower shadow (at least 2x the real body).
  - Little or no upper shadow.
  - Ideally appears after a downtrend or at support.
- Sell an out-of-the-money (OTM) put option with a strike price below the Hammer's low, typically 1-2 standard deviations below current price.
- Choose expiration: nearest weekly or monthly expiration (e.g., 7-30 days to expiration).
- Enter at or near the close of the day the Hammer forms.

EXIT RULES:
- Take profit: Buy back the put when it reaches 50% of the initial credit received.
- Stop loss: Buy back the put if the underlying closes below the Hammer's low by a predetermined amount (e.g., 1-2%) or if the put premium doubles.
- Time-based exit: Close the position at expiration if not already closed. If assigned, manage the resulting stock position (e.g., sell covered calls).
- VIX-based exit: If VIX drops below 15, consider closing early to lock in profits as volatility premium decays.

RISK MANAGEMENT:
- Position sizing: Risk no more than 1-2% of account equity per trade. Since selling puts has unlimited downside risk (until stock goes to zero), use margin or cash-secured puts.
- Diversification: Avoid overconcentration in one underlying or sector.
- Stop loss discipline: Always use a stop loss to prevent large drawdowns.
- Avoid earnings: Do not hold through earnings announcements unless intentionally trading the event.
- Assignment plan: Be prepared to own the stock at the strike price; only sell puts on stocks you are willing to own.

REQUIRED INDICATORS:
- VIX index level (real-time or daily close).
- Candlestick chart with Hammer pattern recognition (can be manual or via scanner).
- Option chain data: strike prices, premiums, implied volatility, open interest, bid-ask spread.
- Support/resistance levels to validate Hammer location.
- Optional: Moving averages (e.g., 50-day, 200-day) to confirm trend context.

ADDITIONAL NOTES:
- Backtest the strategy across different market regimes, as high VIX environments can be volatile and unpredictable.
- Consider using a delta-based strike selection (e.g., sell puts with delta around -0.20 to -0.30).
- Monitor liquidity: Ensure the option has tight spreads and sufficient volume.
- The Hammer pattern is more reliable when it forms at a known support level or after a significant decline.
- Combine with other indicators (e.g., RSI oversold) to increase probability of success.
