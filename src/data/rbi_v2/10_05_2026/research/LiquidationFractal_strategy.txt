STRATEGY_NAME: LiquidationFractal

STRATEGY_DETAILS:

**Core Concept**
This strategy capitalizes on forced-selling/forced-buying cascades in leveraged markets. When a cluster of liquidations occurs, it creates a temporary price dislocation that tends to mean-revert once the cascade exhausts. The edge comes from identifying *where* liquidations cluster (via order book delta + funding extremes) and *when* to enter the reversal (via Fibonacci retracement of the spike).

**1. Key Strategy Components**

- **Liquidation Zone Detection**
  - Monitor order book delta imbalance: track aggressive market sell/buy volume vs. resting liquidity. A delta imbalance > 3:1 with a simultaneous volume spike (>2x 20-period average) signals a liquidation cascade.
  - Identify "liquidation spike range": the high-to-low (or low-to-high) candle/impulse that contains the cascade. Mark this range on the chart.
  - Cluster confirmation: require at least 2–3 consecutive bars with elevated delta imbalance to avoid single-print noise.

- **Funding Rate Extremes**
  - Pull funding rate from perpetual swap markets.
  - Long-side cascade setup: funding rate > +0.05% per 8h (over-leveraged longs) → liquidation spike downward expected.
  - Short-side cascade setup: funding rate < -0.05% per 8h (over-leveraged shorts) → liquidation spike upward expected.
  - Extremes act as a *context filter*, not an entry trigger — they confirm the crowd is one-sided.

- **Fibonacci Retracement Trigger**
  - After the liquidation spike completes, draw Fib levels on the spike range (0% = spike end, 100% = spike start).
  - Entry trigger: price retraces beyond the 61.8% level back toward the pre-spike origin.
  - This ensures the cascade has exhausted and price is genuinely reverting, not just pausing mid-cascade.

**2. Entry Rules**

*Long Reversal (after downward liquidation cascade):*
1. Funding rate was extremely negative OR longs were flushed (delta imbalance heavily sell-side).
2. Downward spike range identified and marked.
3. Price retraces upward and closes above the 61.8% Fib level of the spike.
4. Enter long on the close of the candle that confirms the 61.8% break, OR on a limit at the 61.8% level with a bullish rejection wick.

*Short Reversal (after upward liquidation cascade):*
1. Funding rate was extremely positive OR shorts were flushed.
2. Upward spike range identified and marked.
3. Price retraces downward and closes below the 61.8% Fib level.
4. Enter short on confirmation close or limit at 61.8% with bearish rejection.

**3. Exit Rules**

- **Take Profit 1:** 38.2% Fib of the spike range (partial — 50% of position).
- **Take Profit 2:** 100% retracement back to the spike origin (pre-liquidation price).
- **Trailing stop:** Once TP1 hit, trail stop at the 50% Fib level.
- **Time stop:** If price doesn't reach TP1 within 12–24 bars, exit at market (cascade reversals are fast; slow = invalid).

**4. Risk Management**

- **Stop Loss:** Just beyond the spike extreme (the wick high/low). For longs: stop below the spike low. For shorts: stop above the spike high. This is the invalidation point — if price makes a new extreme, the cascade wasn't done.
- **Position Sizing:** Risk max 1–1.5% of account per trade. Liquidation reversals can be violent; keep size conservative.
- **Max concurrent positions:** 1–2 (correlated assets can cascade together).
- **Avoid:** Low-liquidity sessions, major news events (CPI, FOMC), and assets with <$50M daily volume — liquidation patterns are unreliable there.

**5. Required Indicators / Data**

- Order book delta (bid/ask aggressor imbalance) — via exchange API or aggregated tape.
- Volume (20-period MA for spike detection).
- Funding rate feed (perpetual swaps — Binance, Bybit, OKX).
- Open Interest change (optional confirmation — OI drop + price spike = liquidation).
- Fibonacci retracement tool (61.8%, 50%, 38.2% levels auto-plotted on spike range).
- ATR (for dynamic stop buffer and to filter out spikes that are too small relative to normal volatility — require spike range ≥ 1.5x ATR).

**6. Edge & Failure Modes**

- **Edge:** Liquidations are non-discretionary — forced sellers/buyers must transact regardless of price. This creates predictable overshoots that revert once the cascade ends.
- **Failure mode:** In trending/regime-shift markets, liquidations can cascade *into* a new trend. The 61.8% retracement filter and funding extreme filter help avoid chasing mid-cascade, but a hard stop at the spike extreme is essential.
- **Best conditions:** Range-bound or mean-reverting regimes with elevated leverage. Worst in strong directional breakouts.
