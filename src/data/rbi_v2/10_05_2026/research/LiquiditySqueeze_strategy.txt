STRATEGY_NAME: LiquiditySqueeze

STRATEGY_DETAILS:

**Core Concept:**
This strategy exploits the market's tendency to hunt clustered stop-loss and liquidation zones. When price approaches dense clusters of pending liquidations (visible as liquidity walls in the order book), a breakout is likely as those positions get forced to close, creating a cascade. The strategy uses ATR contraction to time precise entries (low volatility = coiled spring) and ATR expansion to time exits (volatility spike = move exhausted).

**Key Strategy Components:**

1. **Liquidation Cluster Mapping**
   - Pull order book depth data (Level 2/3) at regular intervals
   - Identify price levels where large resting orders + estimated leverage clusters sit
   - Mark "liquidity zones" as price bands (e.g., ±0.3% around a dense level)
   - Rank zones by total notional liquidity (higher = stronger magnet)
   - Refresh zones every 5–15 minutes as book shifts

2. **ATR Regime Detection**
   - Calculate ATR(14) on the entry timeframe (e.g., 5m or 15m)
   - **Contraction phase:** Current ATR < 0.7 × 20-period ATR average → market is coiling
   - **Expansion phase:** Current ATR > 1.3 × 20-period ATR average → breakout is underway
   - Only take entries during contraction; only exit during expansion

3. **Entry Logic (Long example)**
   - Price trades within 0.5% of a high-liquidity zone below current price (short liquidation cluster)
   - ATR is in contraction regime
   - Price breaks and closes above the upper edge of the liquidity zone on the entry timeframe
   - Volume confirms (>1.5× 20-period average) — signals forced liquidations triggering
   - Enter long with stop just below the zone's lower edge

4. **Exit Logic**
   - **Primary:** ATR expands past 1.3× average AND price stalls (bearish rejection candle or momentum divergence) → take profit
   - **Secondary:** Price reaches the next opposing liquidity zone (take-profit magnet)
   - **Hard stop:** Close below zone's lower edge (liquidation cascade failed / fakeout)
   - Optional trailing stop using 1.5 × current ATR once in profit

**Risk Management:**
- Position size = (Account Risk %) ÷ (Entry − Stop distance)
- Max 1–2% risk per trade
- Skip trades if liquidity zone is within 0.3% of a major news event (CPI, FOMC, earnings)
- Maximum 2 concurrent positions to avoid correlated liquidation cascades
- If ATR never expands within N bars (e.g., 20), exit at breakeven — thesis invalidated
- Avoid zones that are too far from current price (>2% away) — low probability of being reached

**Required Indicators & Data:**
- **Order book depth feed** (L2/L3) — for liquidity cluster identification
- **Estimated liquidation data** (from exchange APIs or third-party aggregators like Coinglass)
- **ATR(14)** plus a 20-period ATR moving average for regime classification
- **Volume** (20-period average for confirmation)
- **Timeframe:** 5m or 15m for entries; 1H for higher-timeframe zone context
- Candlestick pattern recognition for exit confirmation (rejection wicks, engulfing)

**Edge & Rationale:**
Liquidation clusters act as self-fulfilling magnets because market makers and large players know where forced selling/buying will occur. ATR contraction signals that the market has absorbed prior volatility and is primed for a directional move — pairing this with a nearby liquidation cluster dramatically increases the probability that the breakout becomes a cascade rather than a fakeout. ATR expansion on exit ensures you leave before the move mean-reverts.
