STRATEGY_NAME: DivergentBandSync

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy combines MACD divergence detection with Bollinger Band positioning and ATR-based profit targeting to capture high-probability reversal setups. The core edge comes from the confluence of momentum divergence (MACD) and price location relative to statistical bands (Bollinger), filtered by a fixed 14-period lookback window. Exits are dynamic and volatility-adjusted via ATR multiples.

1. KEY STRATEGY COMPONENTS
- MACD Divergence Detection: Identify bullish divergence (price makes lower low, MACD makes higher low) and bearish divergence (price makes higher high, MACD makes lower high) over a 14-period lookback.
- Bollinger Band Context: Price must be interacting with the bands for the setup to be valid — bullish divergence near/below the lower band, bearish divergence near/above the upper band.
- ATR-Based Exit: Profit targets are set as multiples of the Average True Range at entry, allowing the strategy to adapt to current volatility.
- 14-Period Lookback: Both divergence and Bollinger calculations use a 14-period window for consistency and responsiveness.

2. ENTRY RULES
Long Entry (Bullish Divergence + Lower Band):
- Over the last 14 periods, price forms a lower low while MACD line (or histogram) forms a higher low.
- At the time of divergence confirmation, price is at or below the lower Bollinger Band (or within a small tolerance, e.g., 0.5% of band width).
- MACD line crosses above its signal line (confirmation trigger) OR histogram flips from negative to positive.
- Enter long on the close of the confirmation candle.

Short Entry (Bearish Divergence + Upper Band):
- Over the last 14 periods, price forms a higher high while MACD line (or histogram) forms a lower high.
- At the time of divergence confirmation, price is at or above the upper Bollinger Band (or within tolerance).
- MACD line crosses below its signal line (confirmation trigger) OR histogram flips from positive to negative.
- Enter short on the close of the confirmation candle.

3. EXIT RULES
Profit Target:
- Long: Exit when price reaches Entry + (ATR_multiple × ATR at entry). Recommended ATR_multiple = 1.5 to 2.5.
- Short: Exit when price reaches Entry − (ATR_multiple × ATR at entry).
- Use ATR(14) calculated at the time of entry and keep it fixed for the trade.

Stop Loss:
- Long: Stop at Entry − (ATR_stop_multiple × ATR at entry), e.g., 1.0 to 1.5 × ATR.
- Short: Stop at Entry + (ATR_stop_multiple × ATR at entry).

Optional Time Stop:
- If neither target nor stop is hit within a set number of bars (e.g., 20–30), exit at market to avoid stagnation.

4. RISK MANAGEMENT
- Position Sizing: Risk a fixed percentage of account equity per trade (e.g., 0.5%–1%). Position size = (Account Risk $) / (Entry − Stop distance).
- Risk-Reward: Aim for at least 1.5:1 by setting profit target ATR multiple ≥ stop ATR multiple.
- Max Concurrent Trades: Limit to 1–3 open positions to avoid correlated exposure.
- Volatility Filter: Avoid entries when ATR is extremely low (choppy) or extremely high (news spikes) relative to its 50-period average.
- Divergence Validity: Require divergence to be confirmed by at least 2 swing points to reduce false signals.

5. REQUIRED INDICATORS
- MACD (12, 26, 9): For divergence detection and crossover confirmation.
- Bollinger Bands (20-period SMA, 2 standard deviations): For price location filter. (Note: 14-period lookback applies to divergence/swing detection; BB uses standard 20/2 unless customized.)
- ATR (14): For profit target, stop loss, and volatility filtering.
- Swing High/Low Detector: To identify the price pivots used in divergence comparison over the 14-period lookback.
- Optional: Volume filter to confirm divergence with declining volume on the extreme move.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
- Divergence detection should compare the most recent swing low/high to the prior swing low/high within the 14-bar window.
- Confirm Bollinger interaction using candle close or wick touch depending on aggressiveness.
- Backtest across multiple timeframes (1H, 4H, Daily) — divergence strategies tend to perform better on higher timeframes.
- Log trades with divergence strength (price delta vs MACD delta) to refine filters over time.
