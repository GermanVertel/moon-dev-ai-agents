STRATEGY_NAME: VolatilityLiquidation

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy combines volatility measurement, trend-following confirmation, and liquidation cascade detection to capture short-term mean-reversion moves after extreme volatility events. The core premise is that forced liquidations (long or short squeezes) create temporary price dislocations that revert once the cascade exhausts, especially when the broader trend context supports a reversal.

1. KEY STRATEGY COMPONENTS
- Volatility Regime Filter: Identify when volatility is abnormally elevated (e.g., ATR or Bollinger Band width in the top 10–15% of its recent range). Only trade during these "extreme volatility" windows.
- Liquidation Detection: Monitor liquidation data (aggregated long/short liquidations from exchanges or on-chain sources). A spike in liquidations — particularly one-sided (all longs or all shorts) — signals a forced-flow event likely to overshoot.
- Trend Context: Use trend-following indicators (EMA slope, ADX, or SuperTrend) to determine the dominant higher-timeframe direction. The reversal trade should align with the prevailing trend (i.e., buy dips in an uptrend after long liquidations; sell rallies in a downtrend after short liquidations).
- Exhaustion Confirmation: Wait for a volatility climax signal — e.g., a large wick, volume spike, or RSI extreme — to confirm the liquidation cascade is losing momentum.

2. ENTRY RULES
Long Entry (fade a long-liquidation cascade in an uptrend):
- Trend filter: Price above 200-EMA and 50-EMA slope positive on the higher timeframe.
- Volatility: ATR(14) > 1.5× its 50-period average, or Bollinger Band width at a multi-day high.
- Liquidation spike: Long liquidations exceed a rolling 95th percentile threshold within the last 1–3 candles.
- Exhaustion: RSI(14) < 30 on the lower timeframe OR a bullish reversal candle (hammer/engulfing) with above-average volume.
- Trigger: Enter on the close of the exhaustion candle or on a break above its high.

Short Entry (fade a short-liquidation cascade in a downtrend):
- Trend filter: Price below 200-EMA and 50-EMA slope negative.
- Volatility: Same elevated ATR/Bollinger condition.
- Liquidation spike: Short liquidations exceed the rolling 95th percentile.
- Exhaustion: RSI(14) > 70 OR a bearish reversal candle with volume spike.
- Trigger: Enter on the close of the exhaustion candle or on a break below its low.

3. EXIT RULES
- Profit Target: Mean-reversion target at the 20-EMA or VWAP on the trading timeframe, or a fixed 1.5–2× ATR move from entry.
- Stop Loss: Placed beyond the liquidation wick extreme (e.g., 0.5–1× ATR beyond the low/high of the cascade candle) to allow for residual volatility.
- Time Stop: Exit if the trade does not reach the target within 3–5 candles, since the reversal edge decays quickly.
- Trailing Option: Once price moves 1× ATR in favor, trail the stop using a 1× ATR chandelier exit to capture extended reversions.

4. RISK MANAGEMENT
- Position Sizing: Risk a fixed percentage (0.5–1%) of equity per trade; halve size during the most extreme volatility decile because slippage and stop-outs widen.
- Max Concurrent Exposure: Limit to 1–2 open positions to avoid correlated liquidation events across assets.
- Volatility-Adjusted Stops: Use ATR-based stops rather than fixed points, since volatility is the entire thesis.
- Regime Kill-Switch: Pause the strategy if realized volatility compresses back to normal (ATR < 1× average) or if liquidation data becomes unavailable/unreliable.
- Slippage Buffer: Assume wider spreads during cascades; require a minimum expected reward-to-risk of 2:1 after fees and slippage.

5. REQUIRED INDICATORS & DATA
- ATR(14) and its 50-period moving average (volatility regime).
- Bollinger Bands (20, 2) width for volatility expansion detection.
- EMA 50 and EMA 200 (trend context).
- RSI(14) on the execution timeframe (exhaustion).
- Volume (for climax confirmation).
- Liquidation feed: aggregated long/short liquidation volume from exchange APIs or on-chain data (e.g., Coinglass, Glassnode, or exchange websockets).
- VWAP (optional profit target reference).

6. EXECUTION NOTES
- Best applied on liquid instruments (BTC, ETH, major FX, index futures) where liquidation data is reliable and spreads are tight.
- Use lower timeframes (1m–15m) for entries but confirm trend on 1h–4h.
- Backtest with realistic slippage models, since the strategy deliberately trades during the most illiquid, volatile moments.
- Avoid trading during scheduled macro events (CPI, FOMC) unless the liquidation signal is exceptionally strong, as news-driven moves may not revert.
