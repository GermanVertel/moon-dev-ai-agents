STRATEGY_NAME: LiquidWave Reversal

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy identifies liquidity-driven reversal points by combining wave-pattern structure with volume dynamics. The core premise is that sharp liquidity sweeps (stop hunts, wick spikes, or false breakouts) often mark exhaustion of a move and precede a reversal. By mapping these events onto Elliott-style wave counts and confirming with volume, the strategy aims to enter reversals at high-probability zones.

1. KEY STRATEGY COMPONENTS
- Liquidity Sweep Detection: Identify candles that pierce a prior swing high/low (or equal highs/lows) and then close back inside the range, leaving a wick. These represent liquidity grabs.
- Wave Pattern Context: Track impulse and corrective wave structures (1-2-3-4-5, A-B-C). Reversals are favored at the end of wave 5, end of wave C, or after a wave 3 extension when momentum diverges.
- Volume Dynamics: Look for volume spikes on the sweep candle (absorption) followed by declining volume on the retest, or a volume climax at the reversal point with a divergence against price.
- Confluence Zones: Combine the sweep with Fibonacci retracement levels (0.618–0.786), prior support/resistance, or order blocks.

2. ENTRY RULES
- Long Entry:
  a. Price sweeps a prior swing low (liquidity grab) and closes back above it.
  b. Wave structure suggests a completed corrective wave (C) or end of an impulse down.
  c. Volume shows a spike on the sweep and a bullish divergence (price lower low, volume/oscillator higher low).
  d. Enter on the close of the reversal candle or on a break above the sweep candle's high.
- Short Entry:
  a. Price sweeps a prior swing high and closes back below it.
  b. Wave structure suggests completed wave 5 or wave C to the upside.
  c. Volume spike on the sweep with bearish divergence.
  d. Enter on close of reversal candle or break below sweep candle's low.

3. EXIT RULES
- Take Profit:
  - First target: prior swing high/low or 1.272–1.618 Fibonacci extension of the reversal leg.
  - Second target: next major wave structure level (e.g., wave 3 or wave 5 projection).
  - Scale out at 50% at first target, trail the rest.
- Stop Loss:
  - Place just beyond the sweep wick (below the low for longs, above the high for shorts).
  - If price closes back beyond the sweep extreme, exit immediately (invalidation).

4. RISK MANAGEMENT
- Risk per trade: 0.5–1% of account equity.
- Position sizing based on distance from entry to stop (ATR-based or fixed pip/point risk).
- Maximum 2–3 concurrent positions to avoid correlated exposure.
- Avoid trading during major news events unless the setup is fully formed.
- Require a minimum reward-to-risk ratio of 2:1.

5. REQUIRED INDICATORS
- Volume (with moving average, e.g., 20-period) to spot spikes and divergences.
- RSI or MACD for divergence confirmation.
- Fibonacci retracement/extension tool for confluence.
- ATR for volatility-based stop sizing.
- Optional: Order block or liquidity heatmap indicator to mark key sweep zones.

6. TIMEFRAMES & MARKETS
- Best on 15m, 1H, and 4H charts for intraday to swing trading.
- Works across forex, crypto, and indices where liquidity sweeps are common.
- Higher timeframe (4H/Daily) wave context improves accuracy.
