STRATEGY_NAME: FractalDivergence

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy combines three orthogonal signals—momentum divergence, chaos/fractal complexity, and volume decay—to catch high-probability trend continuations after pullbacks. It is a trend-following approach that only fires when price structure becomes "complex" (high fractal dimension) while momentum quietly diverges against the pullback, and participation (volume) is drying up.

1. KEY STRATEGY COMPONENTS

- RSI Divergence (Momentum Signal)
  - Compute RSI(14) on the chosen timeframe.
  - Bullish setup (long): Price makes a lower low, but RSI makes a higher low within the recent swing window (lookback 20–50 bars). This indicates weakening selling pressure into the pullback.
  - Bearish setup (short): Price makes a higher high, but RSI makes a lower high.
  - Only accept divergence when the price swing is at least 1.0x ATR(14) in magnitude to avoid noise.

- Fractal Dimension of Price Action (Structure Filter)
  - Use Higuchi Fractal Dimension (HFD) or Katz Fractal Dimension over a rolling window (e.g., 50–100 bars).
  - Compute FD on the price series (log prices preferred).
  - Rule: FD must be INCREASING over the last N bars (e.g., FD_now > FD_5bars_ago) and above a threshold (typically 1.5 for HFD on normalized data).
  - Interpretation: Rising FD = price action is becoming more jagged/complex = pullback is "choppy," often preceding a strong trend resumption.
  - Do NOT take trades when FD is low (<1.3), which indicates smooth, trending, or ranging consolidation.

- Volume Filter (Confirmation)
  - Compute a volume moving average (e.g., SMA(20)).
  - Rule: Current volume and 3-bar average volume must be DECREASING relative to the prior 10-bar average.
  - Interpretation: Pullback occurs on declining volume = weak counter-trend participation = high probability of trend resumption.

- Trend Context Filter
  - Use EMA(50) and EMA(200) on the trading timeframe.
  - Longs only when EMA(50) > EMA(200) and price is above EMA(200).
  - Shorts only when EMA(50) < EMA(200) and price is below EMA(200).
  - This ensures the divergence is a pullback within an established trend, not a reversal.

2. ENTRY RULES

Long Entry (all must be true):
- Trend context: EMA(50) > EMA(200), price > EMA(200).
- Bullish RSI divergence confirmed within last 5 bars.
- Fractal Dimension increasing over last 5 bars AND FD > 1.5.
- Volume declining: SMA(vol,3) < SMA(vol,10) and SMA(vol,10) < SMA(vol,20).
- Trigger: Enter long on close of the bar that confirms divergence (or on break of prior bar's high for confirmation).

Short Entry (all must be true):
- Trend context: EMA(50) < EMA(200), price < EMA(200).
- Bearish RSI divergence confirmed within last 5 bars.
- Fractal Dimension increasing over last 5 bars AND FD > 1.5.
- Volume declining: SMA(vol,3) < SMA(vol,10) and SMA(vol,10) < SMA(vol,20).
- Trigger: Enter short on close of confirming bar (or on break of prior bar's low).

3. EXIT RULES

Primary exits (whichever hits first):
- ATR-based target: Take profit at 2.5x ATR(14) from entry.
- ATR-based stop: Stop loss at 1.5x ATR(14) from entry (below swing low for longs, above swing high for shorts).
- Trailing stop: Once price moves 1.5x ATR in favor, trail stop at 1.0x ATR from the highest high (longs) / lowest low (shorts).

Fibonacci-based exits (optional scale-out):
- Measure the last impulse leg (the swing that preceded the pullback).
- Take partial profit (50%) at the 0.382 retracement of the prior impulse extension.
- Take remaining at the 0.618 extension of the prior impulse (projected target).
- If price closes beyond the 0.786 retracement against the trade, exit full position (structure invalidated).

Time-based exit:
- If neither target nor stop is hit within 30 bars, exit at market to avoid stagnation.

4. RISK MANAGEMENT

- Position sizing: Risk 0.5–1.0% of account equity per trade. Size = (Account × Risk%) / (Entry − Stop).
- Max concurrent positions: 2–3 across uncorrelated markets.
- Max daily loss: 3% of equity → halt trading for the day.
- Max drawdown circuit breaker: 10% → pause strategy, review parameters.
- Correlation filter: Do not hold more than one long in highly correlated instruments.
- Slippage buffer: Add 0.1x ATR to stop distance in low-liquidity sessions.

5. REQUIRED INDICATORS

- RSI(14) — for divergence detection.
- Higuchi Fractal Dimension (HFD) or Katz FD — rolling 50–100 bar window.
- Volume SMA(3), SMA(10), SMA(20) — for volume decay confirmation.
- EMA(50) and EMA(200) — trend context.
- ATR(14) — volatility sizing, stops, and targets.
- Swing high/low detection (fractal pivots, e.g., 5-bar fractals) — for divergence and Fibonacci anchors.
- Fibonacci retracement/extension tool — for scale-out targets.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES

- Timeframe: Best on 1H–4H for swing trading; 15m–1H for intraday.
- Markets: Works best on instruments with clear trending regimes (FX majors, index futures, large-cap crypto).
- Backtest considerations: Use walk-forward optimization for FD threshold and RSI divergence lookback. FD parameters are sensitive—avoid overfitting by keeping thresholds coarse (e.g., 1.5 ± 0.1).
- Signal quality: The triple filter (divergence + rising FD + declining volume) drastically reduces trade frequency but aims to raise win rate and reward-to-risk ratio. Expect fewer than 2–3 signals per week per instrument.
- Failure mode: In choppy ranges, FD stays high and volume may decline—trend context filter (EMA 50/200) is essential to avoid false signals.
