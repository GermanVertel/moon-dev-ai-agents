STRATEGY_NAME: CompressionVolt

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
The CompressionVolt strategy is a volatility expansion system that identifies periods of extreme market compression (low volatility) and then trades the inevitable expansion. It combines Bollinger Band Width (BBW) as a squeeze detector with RSI as a directional filter, and uses the Bollinger Bands themselves as dynamic breakout triggers for exits.

1. Key Strategy Components:
   - **Bollinger Bands (BB):** Standard settings of 20-period SMA and 2 standard deviations.
   - **Bollinger Band Width (BBW):** Calculated as (Upper Band - Lower Band) / Middle Band. A 20-day low in BBW indicates the market is in a tight consolidation, often preceding a significant move.
   - **RSI (Relative Strength Index):** 14-period RSI used to gauge momentum and determine the likely direction of the breakout.
   - **Entry Condition:** The strategy looks for a squeeze (BBW at a 20-day low) combined with an RSI reading that is either overbought (>70) or oversold (<30). This suggests that despite the low volatility, momentum is already leaning strongly in one direction.
   - **Exit Condition:** A breakout beyond the Bollinger Bands. If long, exit when price closes below the lower band; if short, exit when price closes above the upper band. This acts as a trailing stop and profit-taking mechanism.

2. Entry Rules:
   - **Long Entry:** 
     - BBW is at its lowest level in the past 20 days.
     - RSI(14) is above 70.
     - Enter at the close of the candle that meets both conditions.
   - **Short Entry:**
     - BBW is at its lowest level in the past 20 days.
     - RSI(14) is below 30.
     - Enter at the close of the candle that meets both conditions.
   - **Note:** Only one position at a time. No pyramiding.

3. Exit Rules:
   - **Long Exit:** Close the position when the price closes below the Lower Bollinger Band.
   - **Short Exit:** Close the position when the price closes above the Upper Bollinger Band.
   - **Alternative Exit:** If a new squeeze signal occurs in the opposite direction, close the current position and reverse.

4. Risk Management:
   - **Position Sizing:** Risk a fixed percentage of equity per trade (e.g., 1-2%). Calculate position size based on the distance between entry price and the initial stop-loss (which can be set at the opposite Bollinger Band or a fixed ATR multiple).
   - **Stop-Loss:** For long trades, place a stop-loss just below the Lower Bollinger Band or at a 2x ATR below entry. For short trades, place a stop-loss just above the Upper Bollinger Band or at a 2x ATR above entry.
   - **Take-Profit:** The primary exit is the Bollinger Band breakout, but traders may also use a trailing stop or a fixed risk-reward ratio (e.g., 2:1) as an additional profit target.
   - **Avoid Choppy Markets:** The strategy is designed for volatility expansion, so avoid trading during low-volume or range-bound periods where false breakouts are common.

5. Required Indicators:
   - Bollinger Bands (20, 2)
   - Bollinger Band Width (20-period low)
   - RSI (14)
   - Optional: ATR (14) for stop-loss calculation and position sizing.

6. Additional Considerations:
   - **Timeframe:** Works best on higher timeframes (1H, 4H, Daily) to avoid noise.
   - **Confirmation:** Some traders may wait for a candle close beyond the Bollinger Band to confirm the breakout before entering.
   - **Backtesting:** Test on multiple assets and market conditions to ensure robustness. The strategy may perform poorly in strongly trending markets where BBW remains low for extended periods.
   - **False Signals:** The combination of BBW low and RSI extreme can sometimes occur before a false breakout. Using a stop-loss is crucial.

This strategy aims to capture explosive moves following periods of low volatility, using RSI to filter for the most probable direction and Bollinger Bands to manage exits dynamically.
