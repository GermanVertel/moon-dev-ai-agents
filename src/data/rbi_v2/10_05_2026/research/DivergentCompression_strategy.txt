STRATEGY_NAME: DivergentCompression

STRATEGY_DETAILS:

**Core Concept:**
This strategy capitalizes on hidden momentum shifts that occur when MACD diverges from price action during low-volume compression phases, using Bollinger Band breakeven points as the exit trigger. The premise is that low volume during divergence signals weak conviction in the current trend, making a reversal or continuation move likely once volatility expands.

---

**1. Key Strategy Components**

- **MACD Divergence Detection:** Compare MACD line/histogram direction against price highs/lows.
  - *Bullish Divergence:* Price makes a lower low while MACD makes a higher low.
  - *Bearish Divergence:* Price makes a higher high while MACD makes a lower high.
- **Volume Confirmation Filter:** Only take signals when current volume is below its 20-period moving average (low-volume regime). This confirms the divergence is happening in a "compression" phase.
- **Bollinger Band Breakeven Points:** Use BB middle band (SMA 20) or the band edges as volatility-adjusted exit thresholds. "Breakeven point" here = the price level where the trade's risk/reward flips, typically the BB midline or the opposite band.

---

**2. Entry Rules**

**Long Entry (Bullish Divergence):**
- Price forms a lower low while MACD forms a higher low (confirmed on closed candle).
- Volume < 20-period volume SMA.
- Price is near or below the lower Bollinger Band (oversold compression).
- Enter on the close of the confirmation candle.

**Short Entry (Bearish Divergence):**
- Price forms a higher high while MACD forms a lower high.
- Volume < 20-period volume SMA.
- Price is near or above the upper Bollinger Band (overbought compression).
- Enter on the close of the confirmation candle.

---

**3. Exit Rules**

- **Primary Exit (Volatility Breakeven):**
  - Long: Exit when price closes above the BB middle band (SMA 20) — the volatility breakeven point where the move is confirmed.
  - Short: Exit when price closes below the BB middle band.
- **Extended Exit (Band Touch):**
  - Long: Exit when price touches or closes above the upper Bollinger Band (volatility expansion target).
  - Short: Exit when price touches or closes below the lower Bollinger Band.
- **Stop Loss:**
  - Long: Stop below the most recent swing low or 1.5× ATR below entry.
  - Short: Stop above the most recent swing high or 1.5× ATR above entry.
- **Time Stop:** Exit after 10–15 bars if neither target nor stop is hit (divergence thesis invalidated).

---

**4. Required Indicators**

| Indicator | Settings | Purpose |
|-----------|----------|---------|
| MACD | 12, 26, 9 | Divergence detection |
| Volume SMA | 20-period | Low-volume filter |
| Bollinger Bands | 20-period, 2 std dev | Compression + breakeven exits |
| ATR | 14-period | Dynamic stop sizing |

---

**5. Risk Management**

- **Position Sizing:** Risk no more than 1% of equity per trade. Size = (Account × 0.01) / (Entry − Stop).
- **Risk/Reward Minimum:** Only take trades where the distance to the BB midline (breakeven) is at least 1.5× the stop distance.
- **Max Concurrent Trades:** 2–3 to avoid correlated exposure during low-volume regimes.
- **Regime Filter:** Avoid trading during major news events or when volume SMA is rising sharply (contradicts the low-volume premise).
- **Trailing Option:** Once price passes the BB midline, trail stop to breakeven to lock in gains.

---

**6. Why This Works**

Low volume during divergence signals that the prevailing trend lacks participation — a classic setup for a "compression breakout." Bollinger Bands quantify this compression, and the midline acts as a natural volatility breakeven: once price crosses it, momentum has shifted in favor of the trade. Exiting at band touches captures the volatility expansion that follows compression.

**Best Timeframes:** 15m–4H for intraday/swing; avoid 1m (too noisy for divergence).
