STRATEGY_NAME: FluxPivot

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy identifies momentum reversal events by combining MACD zero-line crossovers with order flow indicators. The core idea is to catch shifts in momentum as they happen, using MACD zero-line crosses as the trigger and order flow as the confirmation filter to avoid false signals.

Key Strategy Components:
1. MACD Zero-Line Crossover (Trigger):
   - Use standard MACD settings (12, 26, 9) or a slightly faster variant (8, 21, 5) for more responsive signals.
   - A bullish reversal signal occurs when the MACD line crosses from below to above the zero line.
   - A bearish reversal signal occurs when the MACD line crosses from above to below the zero line.
   - The signal line and histogram are used to confirm the strength of the crossover.

2. Order Flow Confirmation (Filter):
   - Use cumulative delta (the running total of buy volume minus sell volume) or a similar order flow metric.
   - For a bullish reversal, cumulative delta should be rising or turning positive, indicating aggressive buying.
   - For a bearish reversal, cumulative delta should be falling or turning negative, indicating aggressive selling.
   - Optionally, use bid/ask imbalance or volume profile to confirm that buyers/sellers are stepping in at key levels.

3. Entry Rules:
   - Long Entry: MACD line crosses above zero AND cumulative delta is positive or rising over the last N bars (e.g., 5-10 bars).
   - Short Entry: MACD line crosses below zero AND cumulative delta is negative or falling over the last N bars.
   - Enter at the close of the candle that confirms both conditions, or on the next candle open.

4. Exit Rules:
   - Take Profit: Use a fixed risk-reward ratio (e.g., 1.5:1 or 2:1) or trail the stop using an ATR-based method.
   - Stop Loss: Place stop below the recent swing low (for longs) or above the recent swing high (for shorts), or use an ATR multiple (e.g., 1.5x ATR).
   - Exit Signal: Close the position if MACD crosses back below zero (for longs) or above zero (for shorts), or if cumulative delta reverses against the position.

5. Risk Management:
   - Risk no more than 1-2% of account equity per trade.
   - Avoid trading during low-liquidity periods (e.g., late Asian session) to ensure order flow data is reliable.
   - Use a maximum of 2-3 concurrent positions to manage exposure.
   - Consider a time-based stop: exit if the trade does not move in favor within X bars (e.g., 10-15 bars).

6. Required Indicators:
   - MACD (12, 26, 9) with zero line.
   - Cumulative Delta or Volume Delta (order flow indicator).
   - ATR (14) for stop-loss and position sizing.
   - Optional: Volume Profile or Bid/Ask Imbalance for additional confirmation.

7. Timeframes:
   - Best suited for intraday timeframes (5m, 15m, 1h) where order flow data is most meaningful.
   - Can be adapted for swing trading on 4h or daily charts, but order flow confirmation may be less reliable.

8. Market Conditions:
   - Works best in trending or breakout markets where momentum shifts are clear.
   - Avoid ranging or choppy markets where MACD zero-line crosses are frequent and order flow is noisy.

9. Additional Notes:
   - Backtest with realistic order flow data (e.g., footprint charts or tick data) to avoid look-ahead bias.
   - Combine with support/resistance levels to improve signal quality.
   - Consider using a momentum oscillator (e.g., RSI) as a secondary filter to avoid overbought/oversold traps.
