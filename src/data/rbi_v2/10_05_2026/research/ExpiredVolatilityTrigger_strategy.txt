STRATEGY_NAME: ExpiredVolatilityTrigger

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy targets futures contracts that have already passed their expiration date by at least 5 days, exploiting a specific volatility expansion signal to enter a long position. It combines calendar-based contract selection with an intraday range-vs-ATR filter to identify moments of abnormal price movement.

1. KEY STRATEGY COMPONENTS

- Contract Universe: Futures contracts that are 5 or more days past their expiration date. (Note: in most markets these would be settled/rolled, so this assumes a market or instrument where expired contracts remain tradable — e.g., certain crypto perpetuals/dated futures, or a synthetic/backtested scenario. The trader should verify tradability.)
- Position Sizing: Fixed 20% of portfolio equity allocated to long exposure per qualifying signal.
- Direction: Long only.
- Volatility Signal: Compare the current daily High-Low range against the Average True Range (ATR), then compare that spread to a fraction of the contract's daily volatility.

2. ENTRY RULE

Trigger a long entry when BOTH conditions are met:
  a) Contract age condition: days_since_expiration >= 5
  b) Volatility expansion condition:
     (Daily High - Daily Low) - ATR  >=  Daily_Volatility * 0.25

Where:
- Daily High / Daily Low = current session's high and low price.
- ATR = Average True Range over a chosen lookback (commonly 14 periods).
- Daily_Volatility = a measure of the contract's typical daily volatility (e.g., standard deviation of daily returns, or ATR/price, or an implied-vol-based figure). The trader must define this consistently.

Interpretation: The range must exceed ATR by at least 25% of the contract's normal daily volatility, signaling an unusually large directional/intraday move that may continue.

3. EXIT RULES (to be defined/parameterized)

- Take-profit: fixed % gain or ATR-multiple target (e.g., 2x ATR).
- Stop-loss: fixed % loss or 1x ATR below entry.
- Time-based exit: close after N days or when the volatility condition no longer holds.
- Trailing stop: optional ATR-based trailing stop to lock in gains during continued expansion.

4. RISK MANAGEMENT

- Max allocation per trade: 20% of equity (as specified).
- Cap total concurrent long exposure (e.g., no more than 40–60%) to avoid over-concentration.
- Use ATR-based stops so risk scales with volatility.
- Avoid entries during illiquid sessions; expired contracts often have thin liquidity and wide spreads — slippage must be modeled.
- Consider hard stop on days_since_expiration exceeding a max threshold (e.g., 30 days) to avoid dead contracts.

5. REQUIRED INDICATORS / DATA

- Daily High and Low prices.
- ATR (14-period default).
- Daily Volatility metric (std dev of returns, ATR/price, or implied vol).
- Days-since-expiration counter per contract.
- Portfolio equity tracker for 20% sizing.
- Liquidity/spread data to filter tradable contracts.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES

- The core edge: volatility expansion on stale/expired contracts may reflect forced unwinds, settlement anomalies, or renewed speculative interest — a long bias assumes upside continuation.
- Backtest carefully: expired-contract data is often messy; verify data quality and contract survivorship.
- Add a liquidity filter (minimum volume/open interest) since post-expiration contracts can be extremely thin.
- Consider whether "daily volatility" should be realized or implied; the choice materially changes signal frequency.

Summary: A volatility-expansion long strategy on past-expiration futures, gated by a range-vs-ATR spread test scaled by the contract's daily volatility, sized at 20% exposure with ATR-based risk controls.
