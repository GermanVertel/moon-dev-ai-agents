STRATEGY_NAME: Volumetric Divergence

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy combines volatility measurement with price-volume divergence analysis to identify high-probability reversal and continuation setups. The core premise is that when price action and volume patterns diverge (i.e., move in opposite directions or at different rates), the "distance" between their divergence points creates a measurable signal strength that can be traded.

**1. Key Strategy Components**

- **Volatility Baseline:** Use ATR (Average True Range) or Bollinger Band width to establish the current volatility regime. Only take trades when volatility is expanding or at a defined threshold, since divergence signals are more reliable in active markets.
- **Price-Volume Divergence Detection:** 
  - Calculate a normalized price momentum oscillator (e.g., ROC or RSI).
  - Calculate a normalized volume momentum oscillator (e.g., volume ROC or OBV slope).
  - Plot both on a common scale (z-score normalization) to directly compare their movements.
- **Divergence Distance Metric:** The "distance between divergence points" is quantified as the absolute or signed difference between the price oscillator and volume oscillator at any given bar. A large positive distance (price rising, volume falling) suggests weakening trend; a large negative distance (price falling, volume rising) suggests accumulation.
- **Intersection Logic:** Trades trigger when the divergence distance crosses a dynamic threshold derived from its own rolling standard deviation (e.g., 1.5x sigma), confirming a statistically significant divergence.

**2. Entry Rules**

- **Long Entry:** 
  - Price oscillator is negative or turning up from oversold territory.
  - Volume oscillator is positive and rising (volume expanding while price is depressed).
  - Divergence distance crosses above +1.5 standard deviations (volume strength exceeding price weakness).
  - Volatility filter: ATR is above its 20-period average.
- **Short Entry:** 
  - Price oscillator is positive or turning down from overbought territory.
  - Volume oscillator is negative or falling (volume declining while price is elevated).
  - Divergence distance crosses below -1.5 standard deviations.
  - Volatility filter: ATR is above its 20-period average.

**3. Exit Rules**

- **Profit Target:** Exit when the divergence distance reverts to zero (price and volume oscillators realign), indicating the divergence has been resolved.
- **Stop Loss:** Place stop at 1.5x ATR from entry, or exit if the divergence distance moves further against the position by another 1.0 standard deviation (signal invalidation).
- **Time Stop:** Exit after N bars (e.g., 10) if neither target nor stop is hit, to avoid dead trades.

**4. Risk Management**

- Risk no more than 1% of account equity per trade.
- Position size = (Account Risk %) / (Stop Distance in price terms).
- Avoid trading during low-liquidity sessions or when ATR is below its 20-period average (choppy, non-trending conditions produce false divergences).
- Cap simultaneous open positions to 2-3 to limit correlated exposure.

**5. Required Indicators**

- ATR (14-period) for volatility regime and stop placement.
- Normalized Price Oscillator (e.g., 14-period RSI or 10-period ROC, z-scored over 50 bars).
- Normalized Volume Oscillator (e.g., 14-period volume ROC or OBV slope, z-scored over 50 bars).
- Rolling standard deviation of the divergence distance (50-period) for threshold calculation.
- Optional: Bollinger Band width for secondary volatility confirmation.

**Summary:** Volumetric Divergence exploits the statistical gap between price and volume momentum, using volatility as a regime filter and divergence distance as the primary trade trigger. It is most effective in markets where volume leads price (accumulation/distribution phases) and volatility is expanding.
