STRATEGY_NAME: CompressionRider

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy captures explosive directional moves that follow periods of unusually low volatility, using Bollinger Band contraction as the signal that a breakout is imminent. It rides the expansion phase with an ATR-based trailing stop to lock in gains while giving the move room to develop.

CORE LOGIC
Volatility is mean-reverting. When Bollinger Bands squeeze to their tightest width in 20 days, it signals energy building up before a directional release. A close above the upper band confirms the breakout direction (long bias only in this version), and the trade is managed with a trailing stop tied to ATR so the exit adapts to the prevailing volatility regime.

INDICATORS REQUIRED
- Bollinger Bands: 20-period SMA, 2 standard deviations (upper, middle, lower)
- Band Width: (Upper Band − Lower Band) / Middle Band — computed each bar
- 20-day minimum of Band Width (rolling lookback) to identify the squeeze
- ATR: 14-period Average True Range
- Price close data

ENTRY RULES (LONG)
1. Compute Band Width for the current bar.
2. Compute the lowest Band Width value over the trailing 20 bars (including current).
3. Squeeze condition: current Band Width equals the 20-day minimum (or is within a small tolerance, e.g., the 5th percentile of the last 20 values) — this flags the tightest contraction.
4. Breakout confirmation: price closes ABOVE the upper Bollinger Band on the same bar or within 1–2 bars of the squeeze flag.
5. Optional filter: require the close to be above the 20-period middle band and/or require volume above its 20-period average to avoid false breaks.
6. Enter long at the next bar's open (or on the close of the confirmation bar for aggressive execution).

EXIT RULES
1. Initial stop: entry price minus (ATR_multiple × ATR), where ATR_multiple is typically 2.0–3.0.
2. Trailing stop: once the trade moves in favor, trail the stop at (ATR_trail_multiple × ATR) below the highest close (or highest high) reached since entry. Common value: 2.0–2.5 × ATR.
3. The trailing stop only ratchets upward — never lowers it.
4. Optional profit target: exit if price closes back below the middle Bollinger Band, signaling loss of momentum.
5. Exit on stop hit (intrabar) or on a close below the trailing stop level, depending on execution preference.

RISK MANAGEMENT
- Position sizing: risk a fixed fraction of equity per trade (e.g., 1%). Position size = (Account Risk $) / (Entry Price − Initial Stop Price).
- ATR-based stops automatically widen in volatile markets and tighten in calm ones, keeping risk roughly constant in dollar terms.
- Avoid entering if the squeeze is too recent relative to a major news event, or if ATR is already extremely elevated (breakout may be exhausted).
- Cap concurrent positions and avoid correlated instruments to prevent clustered risk.
- Consider a time stop: exit if the trade hasn't reached a defined profit threshold within N bars (e.g., 15), since failed squeezes often chop sideways.

PARAMETERS TO TUNE
- Bollinger period (default 20) and standard deviation multiplier (default 2.0)
- Squeeze lookback (default 20 bars)
- ATR period (default 14)
- Initial stop ATR multiple (default 2.5)
- Trailing stop ATR multiple (default 2.0)
- Optional volume confirmation threshold

RATIONALE
The Bollinger squeeze isolates low-volatility regimes where breakouts have the highest reward-to-risk. Requiring a close above the upper band filters for genuine upside momentum rather than mere noise. The ATR trailing stop is the key differentiator: it lets winners run during volatility expansion while progressively protecting profits, adapting automatically to changing market conditions instead of using a fixed percentage.
