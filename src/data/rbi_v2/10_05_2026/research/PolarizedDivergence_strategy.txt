STRATEGY_NAME: PolarizedDivergence

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy exploits internal momentum conflict between RSI (bearish) and MACD (bullish), confirmed by declining on-bar volume, then exits when price reaches a statistically derived trend reversal zone. ATR risk bands govern position sizing and stop placement.

1. Core Concept
- The setup is a "polarized" momentum state: RSI signals weakening/bearish momentum while MACD signals bullish momentum. This conflict often precedes a short-term exhaustion or reversal.
- Decreasing on-bar volume acts as confirmation that the bearish RSI signal is gaining traction (selling pressure without volume support for the bullish MACD).
- Entry is taken on the divergence confirmation, anticipating a move toward the lower percentile reversal zone.

2. Indicators and Inputs
- RSI (period 14): Bearish condition = RSI < 50 and sloping down, or RSI making lower highs while price makes higher highs.
- MACD (12, 26, 9): Bullish condition = MACD line > signal line, or histogram > 0.
- Volume: On-bar volume decreasing over the last 2–3 bars (current bar volume < prior bar volume < bar before).
- Percentile Levels: Compute rolling percentile bands (e.g., 20th and 80th percentile of price over lookback N=100). The "trend reversal zone" is the lower percentile band (for short entries) or upper percentile band (for long entries).
- ATR (period 14): Used for risk bands (stop loss, take profit, and position sizing).

3. Entry Rules (Short Bias Example)
- RSI bearish: RSI < 50 and RSI[0] < RSI[1].
- MACD bullish: MACD line > Signal line OR MACD histogram > 0.
- Volume decreasing: Volume[0] < Volume[1] < Volume[2].
- Price is above the lower percentile reversal zone (so there is room to fall).
- Enter short at close of the confirmation bar.

4. Exit Rules
- Primary exit: Price crosses into the lower percentile reversal zone (e.g., price <= 20th percentile of lookback N). Close the short.
- Alternative exit: ATR-based stop or target is hit.
- Time-based exit: If neither condition is met within X bars, close at market.

5. Risk Management (ATR Bands)
- Stop Loss: Entry price + (1.5 × ATR) for shorts.
- Take Profit: Entry price − (2.5 × ATR) for shorts, or the percentile zone, whichever is closer.
- Position Sizing: Risk per trade = 1% of equity. Position size = (Equity × 0.01) / (1.5 × ATR).
- Trailing Stop: Optional — trail stop by 1 × ATR once price moves 1 × ATR in favor.

6. Long Bias (Mirror Logic)
- RSI bullish (RSI > 50 and rising) while MACD bearish (MACD line < signal line).
- Volume decreasing.
- Enter long, exit when price crosses into the upper percentile reversal zone.
- ATR bands mirrored: stop = entry − 1.5 × ATR, target = entry + 2.5 × ATR.

7. Confirmation and Filters
- Avoid entries during major news events or low-liquidity sessions.
- Require at least 20 bars of history for percentile and ATR calculations.
- Optional trend filter: Only take shorts if price is below a long-term moving average (e.g., 200 EMA), and longs if above.

8. Pseudocode Summary
```
if RSI < 50 and RSI falling and MACD > Signal and Volume decreasing:
    if price > lower_percentile_zone:
        enter short
        stop = entry + 1.5 * ATR
        target = min(entry - 2.5 * ATR, lower_percentile_zone)
if price <= lower_percentile_zone:
    exit short
```
Mirror for longs.

This strategy combines momentum divergence, volume confirmation, statistical reversal zones, and volatility-adjusted risk for a robust, adaptive trading approach.
