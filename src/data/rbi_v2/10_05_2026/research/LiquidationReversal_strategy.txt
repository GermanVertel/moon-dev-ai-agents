STRATEGY_NAME: LiquidationReversal

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy combines liquidation clustering analysis with Bollinger Band extremes and RSI divergence to capture short-term mean reversion moves. The core thesis is that when forced liquidations cluster near Bollinger Band extremes, it signals capitulation or exhaustion, and a 5-minute RSI divergence confirms the reversal is imminent. Exits target the opposite Bollinger Band.

**1. Key Strategy Components**

- **Liquidation Cluster Detection**: Identify clusters of liquidation events (from exchange liquidation feeds or estimated via large wick/volume spikes) occurring within a tight price zone near the upper or lower Bollinger Band. A "cluster" is defined as multiple liquidation events within a short time window (e.g., 3–5 minutes) and within a narrow price band (e.g., 0.3–0.5% range).
- **Bollinger Band Extremes**: Price must be trading at or beyond the 2-standard-deviation Bollinger Band (20-period SMA, 2σ). This acts as the "extreme" filter for where liquidations are most likely to occur.
- **5-Minute RSI Divergence**: On the 5-minute chart, look for divergence between price and RSI(14):
  - *Bullish divergence*: Price makes a lower low (at/near lower band) while RSI makes a higher low.
  - *Bearish divergence*: Price makes a higher high (at/near upper band) while RSI makes a lower high.
- **Confirmation Timing**: Divergence must form within 1–3 candles after the liquidation cluster is detected.

**2. Entry Rules**

*Long Entry (Bullish Reversal):*
- Price touches or pierces the lower Bollinger Band (2σ).
- A liquidation cluster (long liquidations / forced sells) is detected within that zone.
- A bullish RSI divergence forms on the 5-minute chart.
- Entry on the close of the candle that confirms the divergence (RSI higher low confirmed).

*Short Entry (Bearish Reversal):*
- Price touches or pierces the upper Bollinger Band (2σ).
- A liquidation cluster (short liquidations / forced buys) is detected within that zone.
- A bearish RSI divergence forms on the 5-minute chart.
- Entry on the close of the candle that confirms the divergence.

**3. Exit Rules**

- **Primary Exit (Target)**: Opposite Bollinger Band (lower band for shorts, upper band for longs).
- **Partial Exit**: Optional — scale out 50% at the middle band (20-period SMA) to lock in gains.
- **Time Stop**: If the trade does not reach the target within a predefined window (e.g., 30–60 minutes), exit at market.
- **Stop Loss**: Beyond the extreme wick / liquidation cluster low (for longs) or high (for shorts), with a small buffer (e.g., 0.2–0.3%). A common rule: stop at 1.5× the cluster's price range beyond the entry extreme.

**4. Risk Management**

- **Position Sizing**: Risk no more than 0.5–1% of account equity per trade due to the high volatility of liquidation-driven moves.
- **Max Concurrent Trades**: 1–2 to avoid correlated exposure during broad market liquidation cascades.
- **Volatility Filter**: Skip trades during extreme volatility spikes (e.g., ATR > 3× its 20-period average) unless the divergence is exceptionally clean.
- **News Filter**: Avoid entries during major scheduled news events (CPI, FOMC) where liquidations can cascade without reverting.
- **Stop Loss Discipline**: Hard stop always placed; never widen after entry.

**5. Required Indicators & Data**

- **Bollinger Bands**: 20-period SMA, 2 standard deviations (5-minute chart).
- **RSI**: 14-period on the 5-minute chart.
- **Liquidation Data**: Exchange liquidation feed (e.g., Binance/Bybit liquidations API) or estimated via large wick + volume spike detection if raw data is unavailable.
- **Volume**: To confirm liquidation clusters (spike in volume accompanying the wick).
- **ATR (optional)**: For volatility filtering and dynamic stop sizing.

**6. Practical Notes**

- Best suited for liquid instruments (BTC, ETH, major altcoins) with active derivatives markets.
- Works best in ranging or choppy markets; in strong trending markets, liquidation clusters can continue cascading — the RSI divergence acts as the critical filter to avoid "catching a falling knife."
- Backtest with tick-level liquidation data if possible, since candle-level data may miss the precise cluster location.
