STRATEGY_NAME: VortexSurge

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy combines three complementary technical tools to trade momentum-driven breakouts with dynamic risk management. The core idea is to enter only when a trend reversal/continuation signal (Vortex) is confirmed by strong momentum (CMO) and then protect profits using volatility-adaptive trailing stops (Keltner).

1. KEY STRATEGY COMPONENTS
- Vortex Indicator (VI): Uses VI+ and VI- lines to identify directional trend shifts. A bullish crossover (VI+ crossing above VI-) signals potential upward momentum; a bearish crossover (VI- crossing above VI+) signals downward momentum.
- Chande Momentum Oscillator (CMO): Measures momentum on a scale of -100 to +100. Readings above +60 indicate strong bullish momentum; below -60 indicate strong bearish momentum.
- Keltner Channels: Consist of an EMA (typically 20-period) with upper/lower bands set at ATR multiples (e.g., 2x ATR). The width (upper band − lower band) reflects volatility. Expanding width signals increasing volatility, which is used to adjust the trailing stop distance.

2. ENTRY RULES
- Long Entry:
  - Vortex Indicator: VI+ crosses above VI- (bullish crossover).
  - CMO: Current CMO value > 60 (surge in bullish momentum).
  - Both conditions must occur on the same bar or within a small window (e.g., 1–2 bars) to avoid lag.
- Short Entry (optional, for symmetric strategy):
  - Vortex Indicator: VI- crosses above VI+ (bearish crossover).
  - CMO: Current CMO value < -60 (surge in bearish momentum).

3. EXIT RULES
- Trailing Stop-Loss (Long):
  - Initial stop: Place at the lower Keltner Channel band at entry, or at a fixed multiple of ATR (e.g., 2x ATR) below entry.
  - Trailing logic: As price moves in favor, trail the stop using the Keltner Channel width. Specifically, calculate the current Keltner width (Upper − Lower). If width is expanding (current width > previous width), widen the trailing stop distance (e.g., stop = highest high since entry − 1.5 × current width). If width is contracting, tighten the stop (e.g., stop = highest high since entry − 0.8 × current width). This adapts to volatility: wider stops in volatile markets, tighter stops in calm markets.
  - Exit when price closes below the trailing stop.
- Trailing Stop-Loss (Short):
  - Mirror the long logic: stop = lowest low since entry + (1.5 × width) when expanding, or + (0.8 × width) when contracting.
- Additional Exit: If CMO crosses back below 60 (for longs) or above -60 (for shorts), consider a partial or full exit to lock in profits, especially if Vortex also shows a reversal.

4. RISK MANAGEMENT
- Position Sizing: Risk no more than 1–2% of account equity per trade. Calculate position size based on the distance between entry price and initial stop-loss.
- Maximum Drawdown Control: If the strategy experiences a drawdown exceeding a predefined threshold (e.g., 10%), reduce position size or pause trading until conditions stabilize.
- Volatility Filter: Avoid trading during extremely low volatility (Keltner width near historical lows) or extremely high volatility (width spiking without clear trend) to reduce whipsaws.
- Time Stop: If the trade does not move in favor within a certain number of bars (e.g., 20), consider exiting to free up capital.

5. REQUIRED INDICATORS
- Vortex Indicator: Period 14 (default), with VI+ and VI- lines.
- Chande Momentum Oscillator: Period 14 or 20 (default 14), with threshold at +60/-60.
- Keltner Channels: EMA period 20, ATR period 10, multiplier 2.0 (adjustable). Track width (Upper − Lower) and its rate of change.
- Optional: ATR for initial stop placement and position sizing.

6. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
- Use closing prices for crossover and threshold signals to avoid intrabar noise.
- Backtest across different timeframes (e.g., 15m, 1h, 4h) and asset classes (equities, forex, crypto) to find optimal parameters.
- Consider adding a volume filter: breakout entries are more reliable when accompanied by above-average volume.
- The Keltner width expansion trailing stop is the unique edge—it dynamically adjusts to market volatility, allowing trades to breathe during strong trends while protecting profits during consolidation.

This strategy, named VortexSurge, captures momentum breakouts with a volatility-adaptive trailing stop, aiming to ride trends while managing risk dynamically.
