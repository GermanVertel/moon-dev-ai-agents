STRATEGY_NAME: VolatilityDivergence

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy combines MACD bearish divergence with VIX volatility spikes to identify high-probability short entries, then uses implied volatility dynamics and volume behavior for exits. It is a contrarian-momentum hybrid: price makes higher highs while MACD makes lower highs (bearish divergence), and a VIX spike confirms fear/uncertainty entering the market, increasing the odds of a reversal lower.

1. KEY STRATEGY COMPONENTS
- Primary signal: Bearish MACD divergence on the chosen timeframe (price higher high, MACD lower high).
- Confirmation filter: VIX volatility spike (sharp percentage increase over a short lookback, e.g., +10–20% in 1–3 bars, or VIX crossing above a short-term moving average).
- Direction: Short-only.
- Exit triggers: Rising implied volatility (IV) after entry, or declining volume that signals fading selling pressure.
- Market context: Best in equity index futures/ETFs (ES, SPY, QQQ) where VIX is directly relevant; avoid in low-liquidity instruments.

2. ENTRY RULES (SHORT)
- Identify a bearish MACD divergence:
  - Price forms two swing highs where the second high is higher than the first.
  - MACD line (or MACD histogram) forms two corresponding peaks where the second is lower than the first.
  - Divergence must be confirmed on a closed bar (not intrabar).
- Require VIX spike confirmation:
  - VIX rises by at least X% (e.g., 10%) within N bars (e.g., 3), OR
  - VIX crosses above its short-term SMA (e.g., 5-period) while price is at/near the divergence high.
- Optional filter: RSI below 70 on the second high (avoiding overextended melt-ups) or bearish reversal candle (shooting star, bearish engulfing).
- Enter short at the close of the confirmation bar or on a break below the prior bar’s low.
- Avoid entries within 15 minutes of major scheduled events (FOMC, CPI) unless the strategy is explicitly event-driven.

3. EXIT RULES
- Primary exit: Rising implied volatility after entry.
  - If IV (or VIX as proxy) continues to rise sharply while price does NOT make new lows, exit — this suggests hedging demand rather than directional selling, raising squeeze risk.
  - Concretely: exit if VIX increases by another Y% (e.g., 15%) from entry VIX without a corresponding price decline.
- Volume-decline exit:
  - If volume on down bars declines for M consecutive bars (e.g., 3) while price stalls, exit — selling pressure is fading.
  - Alternatively, exit if volume drops below its rolling average (e.g., 20-period) on a down move.
- Profit target: Optional fixed target at 1.5–3× ATR below entry, or prior support/swing low.
- Time stop: Exit if trade does not reach target within K bars (e.g., 20), as divergence setups decay.
- Stop loss: Above the divergence swing high plus a buffer (e.g., 0.5–1× ATR).

4. RISK MANAGEMENT
- Position sizing: Risk 0.5–1% of equity per trade; volatility-based sizing (smaller size when VIX is elevated).
- Stop placement: Hard stop above the recent swing high; never widen a stop.
- Max concurrent shorts: 1 per instrument to avoid correlated exposure.
- Correlation check: Avoid shorting multiple correlated indices simultaneously.
- Volatility adjustment: Reduce size when VIX is already extremely high (e.g., > 35) due to whipsaw risk.
- Daily loss limit: Stop trading after 2–3 consecutive losses or a 2% daily drawdown.

5. REQUIRED INDICATORS
- MACD (12, 26, 9) — for divergence detection.
- VIX (or IV data) — for spike confirmation and exit trigger.
- Volume — for decline-based exits.
- ATR (14) — for stop and target sizing.
- Optional: RSI (14) for overbought filtering, 5-period SMA on VIX for spike confirmation.
- Chart timeframe: 15m–1H for intraday; 4H–Daily for swing shorts.

6. EXECUTION NOTES
- Confirm all signals on bar close to avoid repainting.
- Divergence detection can be subjective — codify swing high logic (e.g., fractal pivots) for consistency.
- VIX spikes can precede further downside (good for shorts) or mark capitulation lows (bad for shorts); the IV-rise exit rule is designed to protect against the latter.
- Backtest across high-volatility regimes (2008, 2020, 2022) to validate edge.
