STRATEGY_NAME: LiquidSpreadArb

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy targets transient inefficiencies in highly liquid markets by monitoring the bid-ask spread relative to traded volume. The core premise is that in liquid markets, spreads should remain tight; when they widen beyond statistical norms—especially when accompanied by volume imbalances—a short-lived arbitrage opportunity emerges. The strategy does not rely on latency arbitrage (HFT) but on detecting statistically significant dislocations and capturing mean reversion in the spread.

1. KEY STRATEGY COMPONENTS
- Universe: Only assets with high daily volume (e.g., top 50 crypto pairs, major FX pairs, or large-cap equities) where execution slippage is minimal.
- Spread Monitoring: Track the current bid-ask spread (Ask - Bid) and compare against a rolling baseline (e.g., 20-period moving average of spread).
- Volume Imbalance: Compute order book imbalance or trade volume imbalance (buy volume vs sell volume over a short window) to confirm directional pressure.
- Discrepancy Detection: Flag when spread > baseline + k * standard deviation of spread AND volume imbalance exceeds a threshold.
- Mean Reversion Assumption: Liquidity providers will re-tighten spreads, allowing the strategy to exit at a profit.

2. ENTRY RULES
- Long Entry (Buy at Bid, Sell at Ask): When spread is abnormally wide and volume imbalance is skewed to the sell side (indicating temporary oversupply), place a limit buy at the bid. If filled, immediately place a limit sell at the ask (or slightly below) to capture the spread.
- Short Entry (Sell at Ask, Buy at Bid): When spread is abnormally wide and volume imbalance is skewed to the buy side (temporary demand spike), place a limit sell at the ask. If filled, immediately place a limit buy at the bid.
- Entry Confirmation: Require at least two consecutive periods of elevated spread and volume imbalance to avoid false signals.
- Position Sizing: Small, fixed fractional size (e.g., 0.5% of capital per trade) due to the low-profit-per-trade nature.

3. EXIT RULES
- Profit Target: Exit when the spread narrows back to or below the rolling baseline (mean reversion). For a long entry, this means selling at the ask when the ask-bid difference returns to normal.
- Stop Loss: If the spread widens further by a predefined multiple (e.g., 2x the entry spread) or if volume imbalance reverses against the position, exit immediately to limit losses.
- Time Stop: Close the position after a maximum holding period (e.g., 5 minutes for crypto, 1 minute for FX) to avoid tying up capital in a non-reverting spread.
- Partial Exits: If spread narrows partially, scale out 50% and let the remainder run with a trailing stop.

4. RISK MANAGEMENT
- Maximum Concurrent Positions: Limit to 3-5 simultaneous arbitrage trades to avoid overexposure.
- Daily Loss Limit: Stop trading if daily drawdown exceeds 2% of capital.
- Slippage Buffer: Account for transaction costs and slippage; only enter if expected spread capture > 2x estimated costs.
- Liquidity Check: Avoid entering if order book depth at the bid/ask is insufficient to fill the position without moving the price.
- Correlation Risk: Avoid taking multiple positions in highly correlated assets simultaneously.

5. REQUIRED INDICATORS
- Bid-Ask Spread: Real-time best bid and best ask from Level 1 or Level 2 data.
- Rolling Spread Baseline: 20-period simple moving average of spread.
- Spread Standard Deviation: 20-period standard deviation of spread.
- Volume Imbalance: (Buy Volume - Sell Volume) / (Buy Volume + Sell Volume) over a 1-minute window.
- Order Book Depth: Total volume at best bid and best ask (optional but recommended).
- Volatility Filter: ATR (Average True Range) to avoid entering during extreme volatility where spreads may not revert.

6. EXECUTION NOTES
- Use limit orders exclusively to avoid paying the spread.
- Implement a "post-only" flag to ensure maker fees and avoid taker fees.
- Monitor for adverse selection: if the spread widens due to news or a liquidity crisis, do not enter.
- Backtest with tick-level data to capture realistic spread dynamics and fill probabilities.

This strategy is best suited for automated execution in markets with tight nominal spreads and high liquidity, where even small dislocations can be exploited repeatedly.
