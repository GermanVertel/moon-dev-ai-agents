STRATEGY_NAME: TemporalDivergence

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy exploits the term-structure spread between short-term futures and long-term options contracts that exhibit opposing momentum. It is a calendar-spread-style momentum-swap system that goes long near-dated futures while simultaneously shorting longer-dated options on the same underlying, capitalizing on divergence in their price trajectories.

KEY STRATEGY COMPONENTS:
1. Instrument Pairing: Match a short-term futures contract (near expiry, e.g., front-month) with a corresponding long-term options contract (far expiry) on the same underlying asset.
2. Momentum Spread Signal: Compute the momentum of each leg separately. The trade is only valid when the two legs show opposing directional momentum (futures rising while options premium falling, or vice versa).
3. Spread Measurement: Track the price spread (futures price minus options premium) and its rate of change to confirm divergence rather than noise.
4. Regime Filter: Only trade when the spread is widening in the direction that favors the long-futures/short-options structure.

ENTRY RULES:
- Long Entry: Go long the short-term futures contract AND short the long-term options contract when:
  - Short-term futures momentum is positive (e.g., 5-10 period ROC > 0)
  - Long-term options premium momentum is negative (premium decaying or implied vol falling)
  - The spread between the two is expanding upward
  - Confirmation: futures price above short-term EMA (e.g., 20-period) while options premium below its EMA
- Avoid entry if momentum legs are aligned (both up or both down) — that defeats the divergence thesis.

EXIT RULES:
- Profit Target: Close when the spread reverts or momentum convergence occurs (both legs moving in the same direction again).
- Stop Loss: Exit if the futures leg breaks below its recent swing low OR the options leg premium spikes above a defined threshold (momentum reversal against position).
- Time Stop: Exit before the short-term futures contract approaches rollover/expiry to avoid delivery and liquidity risk.
- Trailing Exit: Trail the futures leg with an ATR-based stop to lock in gains during sustained trends.

RISK MANAGEMENT:
- Position Sizing: Size each leg so that notional exposure is balanced; because options have leverage, reduce the options leg size to match delta-equivalent exposure of the futures leg.
- Correlation Check: Only deploy when historical correlation between the two legs is high enough that the spread is mean-reverting, not structurally broken.
- Max Drawdown Cap: Hard stop at a predefined portfolio drawdown (e.g., 2-3% of account) to prevent divergence blowouts.
- Liquidity Filter: Only trade contracts with sufficient open interest and tight bid-ask spreads; avoid illiquid far-dated options.
- Margin Awareness: Account for margin requirements on both futures and short options legs; short options carry unlimited theoretical risk, so cap the short leg with defined-risk structures (e.g., spreads) if needed.

REQUIRED INDICATORS:
- Rate of Change (ROC) or Momentum indicator on both futures and options premium series
- Exponential Moving Averages (EMA) on each leg for trend confirmation
- Average True Range (ATR) for stop placement and volatility sizing
- Spread oscillator (futures price minus options premium, normalized)
- Implied Volatility (IV) and IV rank for the options leg (to detect premium decay/expansion)
- Correlation coefficient between the two legs (rolling window)
- Open interest and volume data for liquidity screening

NOTES:
The core edge is temporal arbitrage of momentum — short-term futures respond faster to news/flow than long-dated options, creating transient divergence. The strategy works best in trending-but-decelerating markets where near-term momentum outpaces longer-dated expectations. It fails in sharp regime shifts where both legs reprice together, so the regime filter and correlation check are critical.
