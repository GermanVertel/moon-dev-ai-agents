STRATEGY_NAME: InverseVolume Divergence

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy combines inverse volume analysis with candlestick breakdown structures, RSI divergence, and stochastic oscillator divergence to identify high-probability reversal or continuation setups. It focuses on detecting when price action diverges from volume behavior, confirming with dual oscillator divergences.

1. Key Strategy Components:
   - Inverse Volume Breakdown Structure: Analyze volume spikes or drops that contradict price movement. For example, a price breakdown with decreasing volume may signal a false breakdown, while a breakdown with increasing volume may confirm strength.
   - Candlestick Patterns: Identify key reversal or continuation patterns (e.g., engulfing, hammer, shooting star, doji) at support/resistance levels or after volume anomalies.
   - RSI Divergence: Look for bullish divergence (price makes lower lows, RSI makes higher lows) or bearish divergence (price makes higher highs, RSI makes lower highs) to signal weakening momentum.
   - Stochastic Oscillator Divergence: Confirm RSI divergence with stochastic divergence (e.g., %K and %D lines showing divergence from price).
   - Confluence: All three elements (inverse volume, candlestick pattern, and dual oscillator divergence) must align for a valid signal.

2. Entry Rules:
   - Long Entry:
     - Price forms a breakdown structure (e.g., breaks below support) but volume decreases (inverse volume), suggesting a false breakdown.
     - A bullish candlestick pattern (e.g., hammer, bullish engulfing) forms at the breakdown point.
     - RSI shows bullish divergence (price lower low, RSI higher low).
     - Stochastic shows bullish divergence (price lower low, stochastic higher low).
     - Enter long on the close of the confirming candlestick or on a break above the candlestick high.
   - Short Entry:
     - Price forms a breakout structure (e.g., breaks above resistance) but volume decreases (inverse volume), suggesting a false breakout.
     - A bearish candlestick pattern (e.g., shooting star, bearish engulfing) forms at the breakout point.
     - RSI shows bearish divergence (price higher high, RSI lower high).
     - Stochastic shows bearish divergence (price higher high, stochastic lower high).
     - Enter short on the close of the confirming candlestick or on a break below the candlestick low.

3. Exit Rules:
   - Profit Target: Set based on recent swing highs/lows or Fibonacci levels. For long, target the next resistance or a 2:1 risk-reward ratio. For short, target the next support or 2:1 risk-reward.
   - Stop Loss: Place below the recent swing low for longs or above the recent swing high for shorts, or use an ATR-based stop (e.g., 1.5x ATR).
   - Trailing Stop: Use a trailing stop (e.g., 1x ATR) after price moves in favor by 1x ATR to lock in profits.
   - Exit on Divergence Failure: If RSI or stochastic divergence fails (e.g., new price low without new oscillator low), exit immediately.

4. Risk Management:
   - Position Sizing: Risk no more than 1-2% of account per trade.
   - Confluence Requirement: Only trade when all three conditions (inverse volume, candlestick pattern, dual divergence) are met to reduce false signals.
   - Avoid News Events: Skip trades during major news releases to avoid volume anomalies.
   - Timeframe: Best suited for 15-minute to 4-hour charts for intraday to swing trading.
   - Backtesting: Validate on historical data with at least 100 trades to ensure statistical significance.

5. Required Indicators:
   - Volume (with moving average to detect anomalies)
   - RSI (period 14, with divergence detection)
   - Stochastic Oscillator (period 14, 3, 3, with divergence detection)
   - Candlestick pattern recognition (manual or automated)
   - ATR (period 14) for stop-loss and trailing stop calculation

This strategy leverages the interplay between volume, price structure, and momentum divergences to capture reversals or continuations with high confluence, reducing false signals and improving win rate.
