STRATEGY_NAME: FiboDivergence

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy combines three technical concepts: RSI divergence, Fibonacci retracement levels, and ATR-based exits. The core idea is to identify momentum shifts (via RSI divergence) that occur at high-probability reversal zones (Fibonacci levels), then manage the trade with volatility-adjusted exits.

**1. Key Strategy Components**

- **Fibonacci Retracement Zones:** Identify recent swing highs and swing lows on the chosen timeframe (e.g., 1H or 4H). Plot Fibonacci levels at 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, and 78.6%. These act as "decision zones" where price is likely to react.
- **RSI Divergence Detection:** Use RSI (period 14) to detect divergence against price action:
  - **Bullish Divergence:** Price makes a lower low, but RSI makes a higher low. This suggests weakening selling pressure.
  - **Bearish Divergence:** Price makes a higher high, but RSI makes a lower high. This suggests weakening buying pressure.
- **Confluence Rule:** Only take a trade when divergence forms *while price is interacting with a Fibonacci level* (touching, rejecting, or closing near it). This filters out low-quality divergence signals that occur in the middle of nowhere.

**2. Entry Rules**

- **Long Entry (Bullish Divergence at Fib Support):**
  - Price pulls back to a Fibonacci support zone (38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, or 78.6% retracement of an uptrend).
  - RSI prints a bullish divergence (higher low vs. price's lower low).
  - Enter long on the close of the confirmation candle (e.g., bullish engulfing, hammer, or close above prior candle high).
- **Short Entry (Bearish Divergence at Fib Resistance):**
  - Price rallies into a Fibonacci resistance zone (retracement of a downtrend).
  - RSI prints a bearish divergence (lower high vs. price's higher high).
  - Enter short on the close of the confirmation candle (e.g., bearish engulfing, shooting star, or close below prior candle low).

**3. Exit Rules**

- **Stop Loss:** Place stop loss beyond the Fibonacci level or the recent swing point that created the divergence. Alternatively, use 1.5x ATR from entry for a volatility-based stop.
- **Take Profit (ATR Multiples):**
  - First target: 2x ATR from entry (partial exit, e.g., 50% of position).
  - Second target: 3x ATR from entry (remaining position).
  - Optional trailing stop: Move stop to breakeven after 1x ATR profit, then trail by 1.5x ATR.
- **Time-Based Exit:** If the trade does not hit target within a set number of bars (e.g., 20 bars), exit at market to avoid stagnation.

**4. Risk Management**

- **Position Sizing:** Risk no more than 1–2% of account equity per trade. Calculate position size based on the distance between entry and stop loss.
- **Risk-Reward:** Ensure minimum 1:2 risk-to-reward ratio before entering. Skip trades where the stop is too wide relative to the ATR-based target.
- **Confluence Filter:** Avoid trades where divergence is weak (e.g., RSI barely deviates) or where price is in a strong trend that ignores Fibonacci levels.
- **Market Condition:** Best suited for ranging or corrective markets. Avoid strong trending markets where Fibonacci levels are easily broken.

**5. Required Indicators**

- **RSI (14):** For divergence detection.
- **Fibonacci Retracement Tool:** For identifying support/resistance zones.
- **ATR (14):** For stop loss and take profit calculations.
- **Candlestick Patterns:** For entry confirmation (engulfing, pin bars, etc.).
- **Optional:** Volume indicator to confirm divergence strength.

**6. Trade Example (Long)**

- Price is in an uptrend, pulls back to the 61.8% Fibonacci level.
- RSI shows a higher low while price makes a lower low at the Fib level.
- A bullish engulfing candle closes above the prior candle high → enter long.
- Stop loss: 1.5x ATR below entry (or below the Fib level).
- Take profit: 2x ATR (partial) and 3x ATR (full).
- Move stop to breakeven after 1x ATR profit.

**7. Common Pitfalls to Avoid**

- Divergence can persist for a long time before reversing — always wait for candle confirmation.
- Fibonacci levels are not exact — use zones, not lines.
- ATR-based exits may be too tight in low-volatility conditions; adjust multipliers based on market regime.

This strategy works best when combined with strict discipline and backtesting on the specific instrument and timeframe being traded.
