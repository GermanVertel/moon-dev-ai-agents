STRATEGY_NAME: VolatilityIgnition

STRATEGY_DETAILS:

This strategy identifies and trades high-probability breakout opportunities by focusing on volatility-driven market conditions. The core idea is that breakouts are most reliable when they occur after a period of volatility contraction, followed by a volatility expansion. The strategy combines volatility analysis, price structure, and momentum confirmation to filter out false breakouts.

**Key Strategy Components:**

1. **Volatility Contraction Detection:** Use the Average True Range (ATR) or Bollinger Band Width to identify periods where volatility has significantly contracted. A contraction is defined as ATR falling below its 20-period moving average by a certain threshold (e.g., ATR < 0.75 * ATR_MA20). This indicates the market is coiling and preparing for a move.

2. **Consolidation Range Identification:** During the contraction phase, define a price range using recent swing highs and lows (e.g., the highest high and lowest low over the last 10-20 bars). This range acts as the breakout zone.

3. **Breakout Trigger:** A breakout is confirmed when price closes outside the consolidation range with strong momentum. Specifically, a long entry is triggered when price closes above the range high, and a short entry when price closes below the range low.

4. **Volume and Momentum Confirmation:** To avoid false breakouts, require volume expansion (e.g., current volume > 1.5 * 20-period average volume) and a momentum indicator like the Relative Strength Index (RSI) crossing above 60 for longs or below 40 for shorts.

5. **Volatility Expansion Filter:** Ensure that ATR begins to rise after the breakout, confirming volatility expansion. This can be checked by ATR > previous ATR value.

**Entry Rules:**

- **Long Entry:**
  - Volatility contraction condition met (ATR < 0.75 * ATR_MA20).
  - Price closes above the consolidation range high.
  - Volume > 1.5 * 20-period average volume.
  - RSI > 60.
  - ATR is increasing (current ATR > previous ATR).

- **Short Entry:**
  - Volatility contraction condition met.
  - Price closes below the consolidation range low.
  - Volume > 1.5 * 20-period average volume.
  - RSI < 40.
  - ATR is increasing.

**Exit Rules:**

- **Profit Target:** Set a target based on a multiple of the ATR at entry (e.g., 2 * ATR) or a fixed risk-reward ratio (e.g., 2:1).
- **Stop Loss:** Place stop loss just inside the consolidation range (e.g., below the range high for longs, above the range low for shorts) or at a distance of 1 * ATR from entry.
- **Trailing Stop:** Once price moves in favor by 1 * ATR, trail the stop using a Chandelier Exit or a moving average (e.g., 20-period EMA).
- **Time-Based Exit:** If the breakout fails to reach the profit target within a certain number of bars (e.g., 10 bars), exit at market.

**Risk Management:**

- Risk no more than 1-2% of account equity per trade.
- Position size calculated based on the distance between entry and stop loss.
- Avoid trading during major news events or low-liquidity sessions.
- Only take breakouts in the direction of the higher-timeframe trend (e.g., use a 200-period EMA on a higher timeframe as a trend filter).

**Required Indicators:**

- Average True Range (ATR) with a 14-period setting and its 20-period moving average.
- Bollinger Bands (20-period, 2 standard deviations) for volatility contraction detection (optional, can use ATR alone).
- Volume with a 20-period moving average.
- Relative Strength Index (RSI) with a 14-period setting.
- 200-period EMA on a higher timeframe for trend direction.
- Chandelier Exit or 20-period EMA for trailing stops.

**Additional Notes:**

- This strategy works best on volatile instruments like forex majors, indices, or cryptocurrencies during active sessions.
- Backtest with different ATR thresholds and volume multipliers to optimize for specific markets.
- Consider using a breakout confirmation candle (e.g., a strong bullish/bearish candle closing near its high/low) to further reduce false signals.
