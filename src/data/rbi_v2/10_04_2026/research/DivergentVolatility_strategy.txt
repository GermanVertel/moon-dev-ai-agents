STRATEGY_NAME: DivergentVolatility

STRATEGY_DETAILS:

**Core Concept**
This strategy combines three complementary technical tools to identify high-probability reversal setups. It enters short positions when RSI signals overbought conditions AND the MACD histogram shows bearish divergence, then exits when ATR-based volatility exceeds predefined thresholds (signaling potential trend exhaustion or a new regime).

**Key Strategy Components**

1. **RSI Overbought Filter**
   - Indicator: RSI (period 14, standard)
   - Condition: RSI > 70 (overbought zone)
   - Purpose: Confirms momentum is stretched to the upside, increasing reversal probability

2. **MACD Histogram Divergence**
   - Indicator: MACD (12, 26, 9)
   - Condition: Price makes a higher high while MACD histogram makes a lower high (bearish divergence)
   - Purpose: Signals weakening bullish momentum despite rising price — a classic reversal warning

3. **ATR Volatility Exit**
   - Indicator: ATR (period 14)
   - Condition: ATR exceeds a predefined multiplier of its rolling average (e.g., ATR > 1.5 × 20-period SMA of ATR)
   - Purpose: Exit when volatility spikes, indicating the move is exhausted or a new regime has begun

**Entry Rules (Short Position)**
- RSI(14) closes above 70
- Price prints a higher high versus the prior swing high
- MACD histogram prints a lower high versus the prior swing high (bearish divergence confirmed on at least two peaks)
- Optional confirmation: bearish candle close or break below short-term EMA (e.g., EMA 9)
- Enter short at the close of the signal candle

**Exit Rules**
- Primary exit: ATR(14) exceeds predefined volatility threshold (e.g., ATR > 1.5× its 20-period SMA)
- Secondary exit: Stop-loss placed above the most recent swing high plus 1× ATR
- Take-profit (optional): Fixed R-multiple (e.g., 2R) or when RSI drops below 30

**Risk Management**
- Position sizing: Risk 1–2% of account equity per trade
- Stop-loss: 1× ATR above entry (dynamic, volatility-adjusted)
- Risk-Reward target: Minimum 1:2
- Max concurrent positions: 1–2 to avoid correlated exposure
- Avoid trading during major news events (high volatility can distort ATR signals)

**Required Indicators**
- RSI (period 14)
- MACD (12, 26, 9) with histogram
- ATR (period 14) plus a 20-period SMA of ATR for the volatility threshold
- Optional: EMA(9) for entry confirmation

**Timeframes**
- Best suited for 1H, 4H, and Daily charts
- Lower timeframes (5m–15m) produce more noise and false divergences

**Strengths**
- Combines momentum (RSI), trend divergence (MACD), and volatility (ATR) for a multi-factor edge
- ATR-based exits adapt to changing market conditions rather than using fixed targets
- Divergence + overbought alignment filters out many weak signals

**Weaknesses**
- Divergences can persist in strong trends (risk of early entry)
- Requires visual/algorithmic swing detection for divergence confirmation
- ATR spikes can trigger exits prematurely in choppy markets

**Best Market Conditions**
- Range-bound or topping markets after extended rallies
- Works poorly in strong, uninterrupted uptrends where RSI stays overbought for long periods
