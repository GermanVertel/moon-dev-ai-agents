STRATEGY_NAME: Volatility-Adjusted Reversion

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy, named "Volatility-Adjusted Reversion," is a mean-reversion approach that uses a normalized price deviation metric to identify overextended moves relative to recent volatility. It enters long positions when price is significantly above its short-term average relative to volatility, and exits when the deviation normalizes or reverses.

1. Key Strategy Components:
   - Mean-Return Ratio (MRR): The core signal. It measures how far price has deviated from its 20-period Simple Moving Average (SMA 20), scaled by the 3-day Average True Range (ATR) to account for volatility.
   - Formula: MRR = (Close - SMA20) / ATR3
   - Entry Threshold: 1.5 (long entry when MRR > 1.5)
   - Exit Threshold: -0.5 (exit when MRR < -0.5)

2. Entry Rules:
   - Calculate SMA20 of closing prices.
   - Calculate ATR over 3 periods (ATR3).
   - Compute MRR = (Close - SMA20) / ATR3.
   - Enter a long position when MRR > 1.5.
   - This implies price is 1.5 ATRs above its 20-period average, signaling a potential overextension that may revert upward (note: this is a long entry on strength, which is unusual for mean reversion—clarify if this is intended as a momentum/breakout entry or if the logic is inverted).

3. Exit Rules:
   - Exit the long position when MRR < -0.5.
   - This means price has fallen to 0.5 ATRs below the SMA20, indicating the deviation has reversed and the trade should be closed to lock in gains or cut losses.

4. Risk Management:
   - Position sizing should be volatility-adjusted: since ATR is used in the signal, size positions inversely to ATR to maintain consistent risk.
   - Use a stop-loss based on ATR (e.g., 2x ATR3 from entry) to limit drawdowns if the reversion fails.
   - Consider a maximum holding period to avoid prolonged exposure if the MRR hovers between thresholds.
   - Monitor for whipsaws: with a 3-day ATR, the signal may be noisy; consider smoothing or confirming with volume.

5. Required Indicators:
   - Simple Moving Average (20-period) on close.
   - Average True Range (3-period).
   - Close price (for MRR calculation).

6. Additional Notes:
   - The strategy as described enters long when price is high relative to its mean (MRR > 1.5) and exits when it is low (MRR < -0.5). This is atypical for mean reversion—typically, mean reversion would short overextensions or buy dips. If the intent is to buy strength and sell weakness, this is more of a momentum strategy.
