STRATEGY_NAME: GradientDivergence

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy combines Bollinger Band slope analysis with MACD momentum to identify trend continuations and reversals, using ATR-normalized thresholds for adaptive exits across varying volatility regimes.

CORE CONCEPT:
The strategy exploits divergence between two independent trend-strength measures:
1. The steepness (slope) of the upper Bollinger Band — a proxy for price trend acceleration and volatility expansion
2. The MACD line — a momentum oscillator reflecting trend velocity

When these two measures diverge meaningfully, it signals either an emerging trend (slope rising faster than MACD) or trend exhaustion (MACD rolling over while band slope remains steep).

INDICATORS REQUIRED:
- Bollinger Bands (period 20, std dev 2.0) — track the upper band specifically
- MACD (12, 26, 9) — use the MACD line (fast EMA minus slow EMA)
- ATR (period 14) — used to normalize all thresholds into volatility-adjusted units

ENTRY RULES:
Long Entry:
- Compute upper BB slope over N bars (e.g., 5), normalized by ATR: BB_slope_ATR = (UpperBB[t] - UpperBB[t-N]) / (N * ATR[t])
- Compute MACD slope over N bars, normalized by ATR: MACD_slope_ATR = (MACD[t] - MACD[t-N]) / (N * ATR[t])
- Enter long when BB_slope_ATR > entry_threshold (e.g., 0.15) AND MACD_slope_ATR > 0 AND divergence = BB_slope_ATR - MACD_slope_ATR > divergence_min (e.g., 0.05)
- This captures cases where volatility/price structure is expanding faster than momentum, signaling early trend initiation

Short Entry:
- Mirror condition: BB_slope_ATR < -entry_threshold AND MACD_slope_ATR < 0 AND divergence magnitude exceeds threshold
- Note: For shorts, use lower Bollinger Band slope for symmetry (or invert upper band logic)

EXIT RULES:
- Exit long when BB_slope_ATR falls below exit_threshold (e.g., 0.05) AND MACD_slope_ATR falls below its exit threshold (e.g., 0.0)
- Both conditions must be met — this ensures the trend has genuinely decelerated rather than a temporary pullback
- Exit short when both slopes rise above their respective (negative) exit thresholds
- Thresholds measured in ATR units make exits volatility-adaptive: in high-ATR regimes, larger raw moves are required before exit

RISK MANAGEMENT:
- Position sizing: risk 1% of equity per trade; stop distance = 2 × ATR from entry
- Hard stop-loss: 2.0 ATR below entry (long) / above entry (short)
- Take-profit: optional trailing stop at 2.5 ATR, or rely on dual-threshold exit
- Max concurrent positions: 1-3 depending on account size
- Avoid trading during low-liquidity sessions; require ATR above a minimum floor to filter dead markets

PARAMETER TUNING NOTES:
- N (slope lookback): 3-10 bars; shorter = more responsive, longer = smoother
- entry_threshold: 0.10-0.25 ATR units — calibrate to instrument
- exit_threshold: 0.03-0.08 ATR units — should be meaningfully lower than entry
- divergence_min: 0.03-0.10 — filters weak signals
- MACD parameters can be adjusted for faster/slower regimes (e.g., 8,17,9 for intraday)

STRATEGY LOGIC SUMMARY:
The dual-slope divergence acts as a confirmation filter. A rising upper band slope alone could be noise; a rising MACD alone could be overbought. Their divergence — band slope outpacing MACD — suggests price structure is leading momentum, which historically precedes sustained trends. The symmetric ATR-normalized exit prevents premature exits during volatility spikes while ensuring timely closure when both signals converge toward zero.
