STRATEGY_NAME: AdaptiveFadeout

STRATEGY_DETAILS:

This strategy identifies false breakouts by measuring whether a price move beyond a key level is supported by sufficient volatility expansion. False breakouts typically occur when price pierces a level but volatility does not confirm the move, leading to a swift reversal back inside the range.

**Core Concept:**
A genuine breakout is usually accompanied by a volatility expansion (e.g., ATR rising, Bollinger Band width widening, or a spike in the volatility index). A false breakout occurs when price breaks a level but volatility remains flat or contracts, signaling a lack of conviction. The strategy fades the breakout when volatility fails to confirm.

**Key Strategy Components:**

1. **Reference Level Detection**
   - Identify recent swing highs/lows or consolidation boundaries using a lookback window (e.g., 20–50 bars).
   - Use Donchian Channels or prior day's high/low as breakout triggers.
   - Track the level that price is attempting to break.

2. **Volatility Confirmation Filter**
   - Calculate ATR (14) and compare current ATR to its 20-period average.
   - Calculate Bollinger Band Width (BBW) = (Upper Band − Lower Band) / Middle Band.
   - A valid breakout requires: Current ATR > 1.2 × ATR average AND BBW expanding vs. prior bar.
   - If volatility fails to expand, the breakout is flagged as a false breakout candidate.

3. **Momentum Divergence Check (optional confirmation)**
   - Use RSI (14) or MACD histogram.
   - False breakout often shows price making a new high/low while RSI fails to confirm (bearish/bullish divergence).

**Entry Rules (Fade the False Breakout):**

- **Short Entry (False Upside Breakout):**
  - Price closes above the upper Donchian/range level.
  - ATR and BBW fail to expand (or contract).
  - RSI shows bearish divergence or is overbought (>70).
  - Enter short on the close of the breakout bar or on the first bearish reversal candle.

- **Long Entry (False Downside Breakout):**
  - Price closes below the lower Donchian/range level.
  - ATR and BBW fail to expand.
  - RSI shows bullish divergence or is oversold (<30).
  - Enter long on the close of the breakout bar or on the first bullish reversal candle.

**Exit Rules:**

- **Take Profit:** Target the opposite side of the range or the mid-band of the Bollinger Bands. Alternatively, use a 1.5× ATR target.
- **Stop Loss:** Place stop 0.5× ATR beyond the breakout extreme (e.g., above the false breakout high for shorts).
- **Time Stop:** Exit if price does not reverse within 3–5 bars, as the breakout may be genuine.
- **Trailing Stop:** Once price moves 1× ATR in favor, trail with 1× ATR or the 20-period EMA.

**Risk Management:**

- Risk per trade: 0.5%–1% of account equity.
- Position size = (Account Risk) / (Entry − Stop Loss).
- Avoid trading during major news events (high volatility spikes can create genuine breakouts).
- Limit to 2–3 false breakout trades per instrument per day.
- Use a volatility regime filter: only trade when VIX or realized volatility is in the lower 50th percentile (choppy markets favor false breakouts).

**Required Indicators:**

- Donchian Channels (20-period) or prior swing high/low levels
- ATR (14) and ATR moving average (20)
- Bollinger Bands (20, 2) with Band Width
- RSI (14) for divergence confirmation
- Optional: Volume profile or VWAP for additional confirmation

**Best Market Conditions:**

- Ranging or choppy markets with low directional momentum.
- Low-volatility regimes where breakouts frequently fail.
- Avoid strong trending markets where breakouts are genuine.

**Summary:**
AdaptiveFadeout exploits the disconnect between price breakouts and volatility expansion. By fading breakouts that lack volatility confirmation, the strategy captures mean-reversion moves back into the range while strictly managing risk through ATR-based stops and position sizing.
