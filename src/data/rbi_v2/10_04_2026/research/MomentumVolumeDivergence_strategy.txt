STRATEGY_NAME: MomentumVolumeDivergence

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy combines EMA divergence with volume confirmation to capture momentum continuations after an initial move, using a trailing stop to ride the trend until it exhausts.

CORE CONCEPT:
The strategy identifies the divergence (spread) between a fast 5-period EMA and a slow 20-period EMA. When this divergence is present AND a significant volume spike confirms an initial directional move, the trader enters in the direction of the divergence (i.e., in the direction the fast EMA has pulled away from the slow EMA). The trade is then managed with a trailing stop, letting winners run while protecting profits.

KEY STRATEGY COMPONENTS:

1. EMA Divergence Detection
   - Fast EMA = 5-period EMA of close
   - Slow EMA = 20-period EMA of close
   - Divergence = Fast EMA − Slow EMA
   - Bullish divergence: Fast EMA > Slow EMA (positive spread)
   - Bearish divergence: Fast EMA < Slow EMA (negative spread)
   - The wider the spread, the stronger the momentum bias.

2. Volume Confirmation
   - Volume MA = 20-period average volume
   - Volume spike threshold = 1.5x to 2.0x the 20-period average volume
   - A "significant volume increase" validates that the initial move has real participation behind it, filtering out low-conviction drift.

3. Initial Move Requirement
   - The fast EMA must have just crossed or pulled away from the slow EMA within the last N bars (e.g., 1–5 bars), confirming a fresh divergence rather than an extended one.
   - This ensures entry occurs near the start of a momentum leg, not at exhaustion.

ENTRY RULES:

Long Entry (Bullish):
- Fast EMA (5) > Slow EMA (20), and the divergence has widened over the last 1–3 bars (fresh bullish divergence).
- Current bar volume ≥ 1.5x the 20-period volume average (significant volume increase).
- Price closes above the fast EMA (confirms the move is holding).
- Enter long on the close of the confirming bar (or on a break of that bar's high).

Short Entry (Bearish):
- Fast EMA (5) < Slow EMA (20), and the divergence has widened over the last 1–3 bars (fresh bearish divergence).
- Current bar volume ≥ 1.5x the 20-period volume average.
- Price closes below the fast EMA.
- Enter short on the close of the confirming bar (or on a break of that bar's low).

EXIT RULES:

Trailing Stop (Primary Exit):
- Long: Trail stop below the lowest low of the last N bars (e.g., 3–5 bars) OR use an ATR-based trailing stop (e.g., 2x ATR(14) below the highest close since entry).
- Short: Trail stop above the highest high of the last N bars OR 2x ATR(14) above the lowest close since entry.
- The trailing stop only moves in the favorable direction and never widens.

Optional Momentum Exit:
- Exit if the EMA divergence collapses (fast EMA crosses back through slow EMA), signaling momentum exhaustion.
- Exit if volume dries up significantly (below the 20-period average) on a continuation bar.

RISK MANAGEMENT:

1. Position Sizing
   - Risk a fixed percentage of account equity per trade (e.g., 1–2%).
   - Position size = (Account Risk $) / (Entry Price − Initial Stop Price).

2. Initial Stop Loss
   - Long: Place initial stop below the recent swing low or entry bar low (whichever is tighter).
   - Short: Place initial stop above the recent swing high or entry bar high.
   - Alternatively, use 1.5x ATR(14) from entry as the initial stop.

3. Trailing Stop Management
   - Activate the trailing stop immediately after entry, or once the trade reaches 1R profit.
   - Use ATR-based trailing (2x ATR) for volatile markets, or bar-based trailing (3–5 bar low/high) for cleaner trends.

4. Trade Filters
   - Avoid trading during low-liquidity sessions (e.g., late Asian session for FX) where volume spikes are unreliable.
   - Require the higher-timeframe trend (e.g., 1H or 4H EMA slope) to align with the trade direction for higher probability.
   - Skip entries when the 5/20 EMA spread is already extremely wide (potential exhaustion).

REQUIRED INDICATORS:
- 5-period EMA (fast)
- 20-period EMA (slow)
- 20-period Volume Moving Average
- ATR(14) for trailing stop and volatility sizing
- Optional: Higher-timeframe EMA (e.g., 50 or 200) for trend filter

SUMMARY LOGIC:
1. Detect fresh divergence between 5-EMA and 20-EMA.
2. Confirm with a volume spike (≥1.5x average) on the initial move.
3. Enter in the direction of the divergence.
4. Manage with a trailing stop (ATR-based or swing-based) until stopped out or momentum collapses.

This approach captures momentum bursts backed by real volume, using the EMA divergence as a directional bias and the trailing stop to maximize the trend's payoff while capping downside risk.
