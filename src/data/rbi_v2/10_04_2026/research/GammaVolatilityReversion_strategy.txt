STRATEGY_NAME: GammaVolatilityReversion

STRATEGY_DETAILS:
This strategy exploits the relationship between dealer gamma hedging flows and implied volatility mean-reversion. It targets stocks with elevated gamma hedge ratios during calm market regimes (low VIX), betting that suppressed implied volatility will revert upward as hedging pressure builds.

CORE THESIS:
When VIX is low (<20), option sellers dominate and implied volatility is compressed. Stocks with high gamma hedge ratios (>1.2) indicate heavy dealer hedging activity, meaning small spot moves force large delta adjustments. This creates a coiled-spring setup: any volatility expansion triggers forced hedging flows that amplify the move, driving IV back toward its long-run mean.

KEY STRATEGY COMPONENTS:

1. UNIVERSE SCREEN
   - Liquid large/mid-cap equities with active options markets
   - Minimum option open interest and daily volume thresholds
   - Exclude stocks with earnings within the holding period (event risk distorts gamma)

2. GAMMA HEDGE RATIO CALCULATION
   - Gamma Hedge Ratio = (Dealer Gamma Exposure) / (Average Daily Volume)
   - Compute net dealer gamma from open interest (assume dealers long calls, short puts as baseline)
   - Flag stocks where ratio > 1.2 (dealers must hedge aggressively per unit of spot movement)
   - Recalculate daily to capture shifts in positioning

3. VOLATILITY REGIME FILTER
   - Entry condition: VIX < 20 (low-volatility regime)
   - Confirm with VIX term structure (contango = complacency)
   - Optional: require stock's IV rank < 30% (relative cheapness)

ENTRY RULES:
- Enter long stock (or long call spreads) when:
  a) VIX < 20 and in contango
  b) Stock's Gamma Hedge Ratio > 1.2
  c) Stock's 30-day IV rank < 30th percentile
  d) Price above 50-day SMA (avoid catching falling knives)
- Position sizing: scale with gamma ratio (higher ratio = larger allocation, capped)

EXIT RULES:
- Profit target: IV expands by 20-30% from entry, or IV rank > 60%
- Time stop: exit after 10-15 trading days if no IV expansion
- Regime stop: exit if VIX > 25 (regime has shifted, thesis exhausted)
- Gamma decay: exit if Gamma Hedge Ratio drops below 0.8 (hedging pressure relieved)
- Trailing stop on stock price: 5-7% below entry

RISK MANAGEMENT:
- Max 5-8% of portfolio per position (gamma setups can gap)
- Cap total exposure to high-gamma names at 30% of portfolio
- Hard stop-loss: 8% on underlying, or if VIX spikes >30% intraday
- Diversify across sectors to avoid correlated vol shocks
- Avoid holding through FOMC, CPI, or major macro events
- Consider defined-risk option structures (call debit spreads) to cap downside

REQUIRED INDICATORS / DATA:
- VIX level and term structure (VIX vs VIX3M)
- Options chain data: open interest, gamma by strike, dealer positioning estimates
- Gamma Hedge Ratio (custom calculation)
- IV rank / IV percentile (30-day and 252-day lookback)
- 50-day SMA for trend filter
- Average Daily Volume (for normalization)

EDGE SOURCE:
The edge comes from the asymmetric payoff of volatility mean-reversion when dealer hedging is concentrated. Low VIX regimes suppress IV, but high gamma ratios signal fragility—when volatility returns, hedging flows create a feedback loop that accelerates the reversion. The strategy monetizes the transition from complacency to volatility expansion.
